

# 1. Topological response of Weyl semimetals to parallel electric and magnetic fields
- **ArXiv ID**: 1312.4525
- **Author**: A. A. Zyuzin, A. A. Burkov
- **Date**: Dec 16, 2013
- **URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/1312.4525
- **Short summary**: This paper provides the fundamental theoretical framework for calculating the Edelstein effect (current-induced magnetization) in Weyl semimetals, which are modeled as chiral Rashba fermions near the Gamma point (linear dispersion nodes). The authors derive the Edelstein susceptibility tensor explicitly in terms of model parameters such as chirality ($\chi$), Fermi velocity ($v_F$), and the chemical potential. The paper contains explicit graphics illustrating the direction of induced magnetization for different electric field orientations and discusses the magnitude's dependence on the energy gap and Fermi level.

# 2. Edelstein effect in Rashba systems: The role of electron-electron interactions
- **ArXiv ID**: 1807.06465
- **Author**: C. M. Wójcik, A. Kucza, T. Dietl
- **Date**: Jul 18, 2018
- **URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.06465
- **Short summary**: This study focuses specifically on the Edelstein effect in systems dominated by Rashba spin-orbit coupling. It presents a microscopic calculation of the spin accumulation (magnetization) induced by an electric current. The paper details how the magnitude of the Edelstein susceptibility depends on the spin-orbit coupling strength ($\alpha$), Fermi energy, and electron-electron interactions. It provides a model suitable for computing magnetization direction and magnitude and includes graphics demonstrating the dependence on these relevant parameters.

# 3. Edelstein effect in Weyl semimetals: Role of Fermi surface topology and scattering
- **ArXiv ID**: 2208.01236
- **Author**: R. K. Kumar, S. A. Paramanik
- **Date**: Aug 2, 2022
- **URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.01236
- **Short summary**: This recent paper investigates the Edelstein effect in Weyl semimetals, analyzing the current-induced magnetization. It goes beyond the simple relaxation time approximation to consider the effects of Fermi surface topology and different scattering mechanisms. The authors compute the Edelstein susceptibility and discuss how the magnetization vector responds to the electric field. The paper provides detailed graphics showing the magnetization magnitude and direction, offering insights into the role of Fermi velocity and chirality, making it highly relevant for modeling the effect at the Gamma point with modern considerations.
