

# Edelstein Effect - Scientific Sources from Arxiv

## Key Papers on Edelstein Effect

### 1. Spin and orbital Edelstein effect in spin-orbit coupled noncentrosymmetric superconductor
- **Authors:** Satoshi Ando, Yukio Tanaka, Mario Cuoco, Luca Chirolli, Maria Teresa Mercaldo
- **Arxiv ID:** 2408.08151
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.08151v2
- **Published:** 2024-08-15
- **Summary:** Superconductors without inversion symmetry can exhibit a non-zero magnetization when a supercurrent is present, leading to non-dissipative magnetoelectric effects. Focuses on understanding the relation between the spin and orbital properties of these effects in conventional spin-singlet noncentrosymmetric superconductors.

### 2. Enhanced Edelstein effect and interdimensional effects in an electron gas with Rashba spin-orbit coupling interface
- **Authors:** A. C. Zulkoskey, R. Dick, K. Tanaka
- **Arxiv ID:** 1912.01804
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/1912.01804v1
- **Published:** 2019-12-04
- **Summary:** Examines the bound-state and free-state contributions to the density of states in a three-dimensional electron gas with a two-dimensional interface with Rashba spin-orbit coupling. Confinement of electrons to the interface is achieved through the inclusion of an attractive potential in the interface.

### 3. Nonlinear spin and orbital Edelstein effect in WTe2
- **Authors:** Xing-Guo Ye, Peng-Fei Zhu, Wen-Zheng Xu, Tong-Yang Zhao, Zhi-Min Liao
- **Arxiv ID:** 2412.02938
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.02938v1
- **Published:** 2024-12-04
- **Summary:** In materials with spin-momentum locked spin textures, such as Rashba states and topological surface states, the current-induced shift of the Fermi contour in the k space leads to spin polarization, known as the Edelstein effect, which depends linearly on the applied current. Studies its nonlinear characteristics.

### 4. Spin Hall and Edelstein effects in a ballistic quantum dot with Rashba spin-orbit coupling
- **Authors:** Alfonso Maiellaro, Francesco Romeo, Mattia Trama, Jacopo Settino, Claudio Guarcello, Carmine Antonio Perroni, Pawel Wójcik, Bartłomiej Szafran, Daniela Stornaiuolo, Marco Salluzzo, Thomas Sand Jespersen, Nicolas Bergeal, Manuel Bibes, Roberta Citro
- **Arxiv ID:** 2602.02036
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.02036v1
- **Published:** 2026-02-02
- **Summary:** Studies spin-resolved transport in a ballistic quantum dot with Rashba spin-orbit coupling, focusing on charge-to-spin conversion and spin Hall effect. In the regime where the dot size is comparable to the Fermi wavelength, identifies a clear crossover from weak localization to weak antilocalization.

### 5. Reactive Hall and Edelstein effects in a tight-binding model with spin-orbit coupling
- **Authors:** Emmanouil K. Kokkinis, Joseph J. Betouras, Xenophon Zotos
- **Arxiv ID:** 2310.11253
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.11253v2
- **Published:** 2023-10-17
- **Summary:** The reactive Hall constant R_H, described by reactive (nondissipative) conductivities, is analyzed within linear response theory in the presence of spin-orbit interaction. Within a two dimensional tight-binding model the effect of van Hove singularities is studied.

### 6. Nonlinear Orbital and Spin Edelstein Effect in Centrosymmetric Metals
- **Authors:** Insu Baek, Seungyun Han, Suik Cheon, Hyun-Woo Lee
- **Arxiv ID:** 2310.05113
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.05113v4
- **Published:** 2023-10-08
- **Summary:** Nonlinear spintronics combines nonlinear dynamics with spintronics, opening up new possibilities beyond linear responses. A recent theoretical work predicts the nonlinear generation of spin density (nonlinear spin Edelstein effect) in centrosymmetric materials.

### 7. Tunable spin and orbital Edelstein effect at (111) LaAlO3/SrTiO3 interface
- **Authors:** Mattia Trama, Vittorio Cataudella, Carmine Antonio Perroni, Francesco Romeo, Roberta Citro
- **Arxiv ID:** 2207.07663
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2207.07663v1
- **Published:** 2022-07-15
- **Summary:** Converting charge current into spin current is one of the main mechanisms exploited in spintronics. One prominent example is the Edelstein effect, namely the generation of a magnetization in response to an external electric field, which can be realized in systems with lack of inversion symmetry.

### 8. Edelstein effects, spin-transfer torque, and spin pumping caused by pristine surface states of topological insulators
- **Authors:** Wei Chen
- **Arxiv ID:** 1901.06953
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/1901.06953v2
- **Published:** 2019-01-21
- **Summary:** The Edelstein effect caused by the pristine surface states of three-dimensional topological insulators is investigated by means of a semiclassical approach. The combined effect of random impurity scattering and the spin-momentum locking of the gapless Dirac cone yields a current-induced surface spin polarization.

### 9. Spin-orbit coupling and the Edelstein effect at conducting ferroelectric domain walls
- **Authors:** Maryam A. Nasir, W. A. Atkinson
- **Arxiv ID:** 2510.03406
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.03406v1
- **Published:** 2025-10-03
- **Summary:** Head-to-head ferroelectric domain walls are intrinsically charged, and are typically compensated by a mix of oppositely charged defects and free electrons. The free electrons form a two-dimensional electron gas (2DEG) along the domain wall. In many cases, inversion symmetry is broken at the wall, which enables the Edelstein effect.

### 10. Spin and Orbital Edelstein effect in gated monolayer transition metal dichalcogenides
- **Authors:** Tapesh Gautam, S. Satpathy
- **Arxiv ID:** 2509.25634
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.25634v1
- **Published:** 2025-09-30
- **Summary:** The Edelstein effect consists of the non-equilibrium accumulation of magnetization in response to an applied electric field in systems with broken inversion symmetry. While the spin Edelstein effect (SEE), originating from spin moments, is well established, its orbital counterpart, where magnetization originates from orbital moments, is less understood.

### 11. Spin and orbital Edelstein effect in a bilayer system with Rashba interaction
- **Authors:** Sergio Leiva M., Jürgen Henk, Ingrid Mertig, Annika Johansson
- **Arxiv ID:** 2307.02872
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.02872v2
- **Published:** 2023-07-06
- **Summary:** The spin Edelstein effect has proven to be a promising phenomenon to generate spin polarization from a charge current in systems without inversion symmetry. In recent years, a current-induced orbital magnetization, called orbital Edelstein effect, has been predicted for various systems with broken inversion symmetry.

### 12. Resonant Edelstein and inverse-Edelstein effects, charge-to-spin conversion, and spin pumping from chiral-spin modes
- **Authors:** Mojdeh Saleh, Abhishek Kumar, Dmitrii L. Maslov, Saurabh Maiti
- **Arxiv ID:** 2501.15752
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.15752v2
- **Published:** 2025-01-27
- **Summary:** Spin-orbit coupling in systems with broken inversion symmetry gives rise to the Edelstein effect, which is the spin polarization induced by an electric field or current, and the inverse-Edelstein effect (also known as the spin-galvanic effect), which is the electric current induced by an oscillatory magnetic field.

### 13. Orbital Edelstein effect from density-wave order
- **Authors:** Geremia Massarelli, Bryce Wu, Arun Paramekanti
- **Arxiv ID:** 1904.04280
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/1904.04280v1
- **Published:** 2019-04-08
- **Summary:** Coupling between charge and spin, and magnetoelectric effects more generally, have been an area of great interest for several years, with the sought-after ability to control magnetic degrees of freedom via charge currents serving as an impetus. The orbital Edelstein effect (OEE) is a kinetic magnetoelectric effect.

### 14. The Edelstein effect in the presence of impurity spin-orbit scattering
- **Authors:** Amin Maleki, Roberto Raimondi, Ka Shen
- **Arxiv ID:** 1610.08258
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/1610.08258v1
- **Published:** 2016-10-26
- **Summary:** In this paper we study the current-induced spin polarization in a two-dimensional electron gas, known also as the Edelstein effect. Compared to previous treatments, we consider both the Rashba and Dresselhaus spin-orbit interaction as well as the spin-orbit interaction from impurity scattering.

- **Rashba spin-orbit coupling** as a key mechanism for the effect

- **Inverse Edelstein effect** (spin-galvanic effect)

These sources provide comprehensive scientific coverage of the Edelstein Effect phenomenon with mathematical formulations, theoretical models, and experimental predictions.
