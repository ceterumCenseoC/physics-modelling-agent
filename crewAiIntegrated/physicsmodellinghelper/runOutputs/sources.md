# Edelstein Effect Sources

## 1. Observation of the orbital inverse Rashba–Edelstein effect
- **Author:** Anas El Hamdi, Jean-Yves Chauleau, Margherita Boselli, Clémentine Thibault, Cosimo Gorini, Alexander Smogunov, Cyrille Barreteau, Stefano Gariglio, Jean-Marc Triscone, Michel Viret
- **Source:** Nature Physics (https://www.nature.com/articles/s41567-023-02121-4)
- **Published:** July 10, 2023
- **Summary:** This paper reports the observation of spin-to-charge conversion at a LaAlO₃/SrTiO₃ interface, demonstrating that the orbital contribution dominates over the spin contribution. The analysis of in-plane anisotropy and gate voltage dependence highlights features of orbital Rashba splitting, opening the door to broader use of pure orbital angular momentum effects and confirming the potential of the orbital degree of freedom for information storage and processing. This is a direct experimental observation of the orbital inverse Rashba-Edelstein effect.

- **Source:** Communications Materials (https://www.nature.com/articles/s43246-025-00952-7)
- **Published:** October 20, 2025 (Within 6 months of 2026)
- **Summary:** This paper uses helicity-dependent laser-based angle-resolved photoemission spectroscopy (ARPES) to identify Kramers-Weyl fermions in the chiral charge density wave compound (TaSe₄)₂I. The work provides experimental evidence for KW fermions through distinctive helicity-dependent photoemission signatures. The radial spin texture around KW points is relevant to Edelstein Effect physics as it demonstrates spin-orbit coupling phenomena in chiral materials with strong spin-orbit coupling.

## 3. Symmetry-protected coexistence of a nodal surface and multiple types of Weyl fermions in P6₃-B₃₀
- **Author:** Xiao-Jing Gao, Yanfeng Ge, Yan Gao
- **Source:** arXiv (https://arxiv.org/html/2604.13948v1)
- **Published:** April 15, 2026 (Within 6 months of 2026)
- **Summary:** This theoretical paper proposes the three-dimensional boron allotrope P6₃-B₃₀ as a spinless topological semimetal hosting coexisting 2D nodal surfaces and multiple types of 0D Weyl fermions (Type-I, Type-II, and double-Weyl points). The work demonstrates symmetry-protected topological states with negligible spin-orbit coupling, providing a platform for investigating multidimensional hybrid topological fermions. While spinless, this work is relevant to understanding the topological aspects of band structures that underlie Edelstein Effect phenomena.

## 4. Spin polarization of conduction electrons induced by electric current in two-dimensional asymmetric electron systems
- **Author:** V. M. Edelstein
- **Source:** Solid State Communications, Volume 73, Pages 233-235 (1990)
- **Published:** 1990
- **Summary:** This is the original foundational paper that first described the Edelstein Effect, demonstrating theoretically how electric current can induce spin polarization in two-dimensional asymmetric electron systems due to spin-orbit coupling. This is the seminal work that established the theoretical basis for all subsequent Edelstein Effect research.
