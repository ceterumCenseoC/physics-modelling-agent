

# Edelstein-Effect Sources for Rashba Fermion Modeling

## Top 3 Most Relevant Papers

### 1. Edelstein Effect in Isotropic and Anisotropic Rashba Models
- **ArXiv ID:** 2503.20712
- **Authors:** Irene Gaiardoni, Mattia Trama, Alfonso Maiellaro, Claudio Guarcello, Francesco Romeo, Roberta Citro
- **Date:** 2025-03-26
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.20712v1
- **Summary:** This paper directly investigates spin-to-charge conversion via the Edelstein effect in a 2D Rashba electron gas using the semiclassical Boltzmann approach. It analyzes the magnetization arising from the direct Edelstein effect, taking into account an anisotropic Rashba model. The study examines how this effect depends on the effective mass, Rashba coupling strength, and Fermi energy, which are crucial parameters for modeling the Edelstein effect at the Gamma point. This is the most relevant source for calculating magnetization magnitude and direction under applied electric fields in Rashba systems.

### 2. Out-of-plane Edelstein effects: Electric-field induced magnetization in p-wave magnets
- **ArXiv ID:** 2501.01888
- **Authors:** Motohiko Ezawa
- **Date:** 2025-01-03
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.01888v1
- **Summary:** This paper specifically studies electric-field induced magnetization in systems with Rashba spin-orbit interaction. It analytically and numerically investigates how out-of-plane magnetization can be induced by the Edelstein effect. The work addresses the direction and magnitude of magnetization under different electric field configurations, which is directly applicable to computing magnetization for various applied electric field directions and magnitudes. The analytical approach provides explicit formulas for the Edelstein effect.

### 3. Theory of the nonlinear Rashba-Edelstein effect
- **ArXiv ID:** 1506.08330
- **Authors:** Giovanni Vignale, I. V. Tokatly
- **Date:** 2015-06-27
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/1506.08330v1
- **Summary:** This foundational paper discusses the current-driven spin polarization in a two-dimensional electron gas with Rashba spin-orbit coupling (the Edelstein effect). It extends the analysis beyond the linear response regime to include nonlinear effects. The paper provides theoretical framework for understanding how spin polarization depends on drift velocity, which is related to the applied electric field. This is essential for understanding how the Edelstein effect depends on relevant parameters like Fermi velocity and chirality.

- **Date:** 2026-01-05

- **Summary:** Uses semiclassical Boltzmann framework to derive analytical expressions for charge and spin currents in Rashba 2DEG. Provides explicit parameter dependencies that can inform modeling of the Edelstein effect.

- **Date:** 2018-05-15
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/1805.05523v1
- **Summary:** Explains how electrons moving in an electric field experience a momentum dependent magnetic field that couples to electron spin, generating spin polarization from charge current (Edelstein effect). Provides experimental context for theoretical modeling.

## Key Parameters for Model Building

Based on these sources, the following parameters are critical for modeling the Edelstein effect in Rashba fermions:

- **Rashba coupling strength ($\alpha_R$):** Determines the spin-orbit interaction magnitude
- **Fermi velocity ($v_F$):** Affects the response to applied electric fields
- **Chirality:** Influences the direction of induced magnetization
- **Fermi energy ($E_F$):** Determines the electronic states available for spin polarization
- **Electric field magnitude and direction:** Directly controls the induced magnetization
- **Effective mass ($m^*$):** Affects the dispersion relation and response functions
- **Scattering time ($\tau$):** Determines the relaxation processes in the system

These papers provide the theoretical foundation for calculating the Edelstein effect at the Gamma point of the Brillouin zone, including explicit formulas for magnetization magnitude and direction as functions of applied electric field and model parameters.
