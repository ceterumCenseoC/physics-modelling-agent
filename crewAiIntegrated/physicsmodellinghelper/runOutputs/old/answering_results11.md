

# Edelstein Effect Calculation for Rashba Fermions at the Gamma Point

## **Executive Summary**

The Edelstein effect describes the generation of spin polarization (magnetization) in a material with spin-orbit coupling when an electric field is applied. For a 2D Rashba fermion system at the Gamma point, this effect produces a magnetization that is **perpendicular to both the applied electric field and the system plane**.

---

## **1. Theoretical Framework**

### **1.1 Rashba Hamiltonian**

The system is described by the Rashba Hamiltonian:

$$
\hat{H} = \frac{p^2}{2m} + \alpha_R (\hat{z} \cdot (p \times \vec{\sigma}))
$$

where:
- $m$ = effective carrier mass
- $\alpha_R$ = Rashba coupling strength
- $\hat{z}$ = unit vector perpendicular to the 2D plane
- $p$ = momentum operator
- $\vec{\sigma}$ = vector of Pauli matrices

### **1.2 Energy Dispersion**

The spectrum consists of two chiral bands with helicity $\nu = \pm 1$:

$$
\epsilon^\nu_k = \frac{k^2}{2m} + \nu k \alpha_R
$$

---

## **2. Magnetization Calculation**

### **2.1 General Formula**

The magnetization (spin density) $\mathbf{M}$ is generated by the applied electric field $\mathbf{E}$:

$$
\mathbf{M} = -\mu_b \sum_{k,\nu} |e| (\vec{\nu}_\nu(k) \cdot \mathbf{E}) \delta [E_\nu(k) - E_F] \langle \vec{\sigma} \rangle^\nu_k
$$

where:
- $\mu_b$ = Bohr magneton
- $e$ = elementary charge
- $\tau$ = relaxation time
- $\langle \vec{\sigma} \rangle^\nu_k$ = spin expectation value

### **2.2 Spin Expectation Value**

The spin texture is helical and locked to momentum:

$$
\langle \vec{\sigma} \rangle^\pm_k = \frac{1}{k} \begin{pmatrix} \pm k_y \\ \mp k_x \\ 0 \end{pmatrix}
$$

This confirms the spin polarization is **in-plane and perpendicular to the momentum vector $\mathbf{k}$**.

---

## **3. Magnetization Magnitude and Direction**

### **3.1 Vector Form**

The magnetization follows this general relationship:

$$
\mathbf{M} \propto [\hat{z} \times \mathbf{E}]
$$

**Key Result:** The magnetization lies **in the plane of the 2D system** and is **perpendicular to the applied electric field**.

### **3.2 Specific Cases**

#### **Case 1: Electric Field Along $\hat{x}$ Direction**

For $\mathbf{E} = E_x \hat{x}$:

$$
\mathbf{M} = M_y \hat{y} \quad \text{where} \quad M_y = \frac{\mu_b |e| \tau}{2\pi} \times \begin{cases} m \alpha_R & \text{(HDR)} \\ \sqrt{m^2 \alpha_R^2 + 2m E_F} & \text{(LDR)} \end{cases} \times E_x
$$

#### **Case 2: Electric Field Along $\hat{y}$ Direction**

For $\mathbf{E} = E_y \hat{y}$:

$$
\mathbf{M} = M_x \hat{x} \quad \text{where} \quad M_x = -\frac{\mu_b |e| \tau}{2\pi} \times \begin{cases} m \alpha_R & \text{(HDR)} \\ \sqrt{m^2 \alpha_R^2 + 2m E_F} & \text{(LDR)} \end{cases} \times E_y
$$

#### **Case 3: Electric Field at Angle $\theta$**

For $\mathbf{E} = E (\cos\theta \hat{x} + \sin\theta \hat{y})$:

$$
\mathbf{M} = \frac{\mu_b |e| \tau}{2\pi} \times \begin{cases} m \alpha_R \\ \sqrt{m^2 \alpha_R^2 + 2m E_F} \end{cases} \times E (-\sin\theta \hat{x} + \cos\theta \hat{y})
$$

---

## **4. Parameter Dependencies**

### **4.1 High-Density Regime (HDR, $E_F \geq 0$)**

| Parameter | Dependence | Effect on Magnetization |
|-----------|------------|------------------------|
| Rashba Coupling $\alpha_R$ | Linear ($M \propto \alpha_R$) | Stronger SOC → larger magnetization |
| Fermi Energy $E_F$ | **Independent** | No change with doping |
| Relaxation Time $\tau$ | Linear ($M \propto \tau$) | Longer lifetime → larger magnetization |
| Effective Mass $m$ | Linear ($M \propto m$) | Heavier carriers → larger magnetization |
| Chirality $\nu = \pm 1$ | Sum over both bands | Both chiralities contribute |

### **4.2 Low-Density Regime (LDR, $E_F < 0$)**

| Parameter | Dependence | Effect on Magnetization |
|-----------|------------|------------------------|
| Rashba Coupling $\alpha_R$ | Square root ($M \propto \sqrt{m^2 \alpha_R^2 + 2m E_F}$) | Nonlinear dependence |
| Fermi Energy $E_F$ | Square root ($M \propto \sqrt{E_F}$) | Magnetization increases with $E_F$ |
| Relaxation Time $\tau$ | Linear ($M \propto \tau$) | Same as HDR |
| Effective Mass $m$ | Complex | Appears in prefactor and square root |
| Chirality $\nu = \pm 1$ | Sum over both bands | Both chiralities contribute |

### **4.3 Fermi Velocity Dependence**

The group velocity determines the current contribution:

$$
v^\nu_F = \frac{k^\nu_F}{m} + \nu \alpha_R
$$

where the Fermi momenta are:

- **HDR:** $k^\nu_F = -\nu k_0 + \sqrt{k_0^2 + 2m E_F}$ with $k_0 = \alpha_R m$
- **LDR:** $k^\nu_F = k_0 - \eta \sqrt{k_0^2 + 2m E_F}$ with $\eta = \pm$

---

## **5. Chirality Effects**

### **5.1 Helicity Index**

The chirality $\nu = \pm 1$ determines:
- The Fermi momenta for each band
- The spin expectation value direction
- The contribution to total magnetization

### **5.2 Spin Texture Direction**

For each chirality:
- $\nu = +1$: Spin points in direction $(k_y, -k_x, 0)$
- $\nu = -1$: Spin points in direction $(-k_y, k_x, 0)$

The total magnetization is the **sum over both chiral bands**.

## **6. Computational Implementation**

### **6.1 Model Inputs**

```python
# Required input parameters
E_field = {'magnitude': 1.0, 'direction': 'x'}  # Electric field
alpha_R = 0.5  # Rashba coupling strength (eV·Å)
m = 0.067  # Effective mass (in electron mass units)
E_F = 0.1  # Fermi energy (eV)
tau = 1e-12  # Relaxation time (s)
```

### **6.2 Calculation Function**

```python
import numpy as np

def calculate_edelstein_magnetization(E, alpha_R, m, E_F, tau):
    """
    Calculate Edelstein effect magnetization for Rashba fermions.
    
    Parameters:
    - E: Electric field magnitude (V/m)
    - alpha_R: Rashba coupling strength (eV·Å)
    - m: Effective mass (kg)
    - E_F: Fermi energy (J)
    - tau: Relaxation time (s)
    
    Returns:
    - M: Magnetization magnitude (A/m)
    - direction: Magnetization direction ('x', 'y', or 'z')
    """
    mu_b = 9.274e-24  # Bohr magneton (J/T)
    e = 1.602e-19     # Elementary charge (C)
    
    # Convert units if needed
    alpha_R_Jm = alpha_R * 1.602e-19 * 1e-10  # Convert eV·Å to J·m
    
    # Determine regime
    if E_F >= 0:
        # High-Density Regime
        M = (mu_b * abs(e) * tau) / (2 * np.pi) * m * alpha_R_Jm * E
        direction = 'perpendicular_to_E_in_plane'
    else:
        # Low-Density Regime
        M = (mu_b * abs(e) * tau) / (2 * np.pi) * np.sqrt(m**2 * alpha_R_Jm**2 + 2 * m * E_F) * E
        direction = 'perpendicular_to_E_in_plane'
    
    return M, direction
```

### **6.3 Example Calculations**

| Electric Field | Magnitude | Magnetization Direction | Magnitude (HDR) | Magnitude (LDR) |
|---------------|-----------|------------------------|-----------------|-----------------|
| $\mathbf{E} = E_x \hat{x}$ | $E_x$ | $\hat{y}$ | $M \propto m \alpha_R E_x$ | $M \propto \sqrt{m^2 \alpha_R^2 + 2m E_F} E_x$ |
| $\mathbf{E} = E_y \hat{y}$ | $E_y$ | $-\hat{x}$ | $M \propto m \alpha_R E_y$ | $M \propto \sqrt{m^2 \alpha_R^2 + 2m E_F} E_y$ |
| $\mathbf{E} = E (\cos\theta \hat{x} + \sin\theta \hat{y})$ | $E$ | $E (-\sin\theta \hat{x} + \cos\theta \hat{y})$ | Same | Same |

## **7. Key Results Summary**

### **7.1 Magnetization Magnitude**

$$
|\mathbf{M}| = \frac{\mu_b |e| \tau}{2\pi} \times \begin{cases} m \alpha_R & \text{(HDR, } E_F \geq 0) \\ \sqrt{m^2 \alpha_R^2 + 2m E_F} & \text{(LDR, } E_F < 0) \end{cases} \times |\mathbf{E}|
$$

### **7.2 Magnetization Direction**

$$
\mathbf{M} \parallel [\hat{z} \times \mathbf{E}]
$$

- **In-plane**: Magnetization lies within the 2D Rashba system plane
- **Perpendicular to E**: Magnetization is always perpendicular to the applied electric field
- **Clockwise/Counter-clockwise**: Determined by the sign of $\alpha_R$ and chirality

### **7.3 Parameter Sensitivity**

| Parameter | Sensitivity | Notes |
|-----------|-------------|-------|
| Electric Field $E$ | Linear | $M \propto E$ |
| Rashba Coupling $\alpha_R$ | Linear (HDR) / Square Root (LDR) | Stronger SOC → larger effect |
| Relaxation Time $\tau$ | Linear | Longer lifetime → larger effect |
| Fermi Energy $E_F$ | None (HDR) / Square Root (LDR) | Regime-dependent |
| Chirality $\nu$ | Sum over bands | Both contribute to total M |
| Fermi Velocity $v_F$ | Indirect | Through $k_F$ and $k_0$ |

---

## **8. Conclusions**

1. **Direction**: The Edelstein magnetization is always **perpendicular to the applied electric field** and lies **in the plane** of the 2D Rashba system.

2. **Magnitude**: Scales **linearly with electric field strength** and **relaxation time**, with regime-dependent dependence on Rashba coupling and Fermi energy.

3. **Chirality**: Both chiral bands ($\nu = \pm 1$) contribute to the total magnetization, with their contributions determined by the Fermi momenta and spin expectation values.

4. **Regime Dependence**: The High-Density Regime shows constant magnetization independent of $E_F$, while the Low-Density Regime shows square-root dependence on $E_F$.

5. **Fermi Velocity**: Affects the current-spin conversion efficiency through the group velocity $v^\nu_F = \frac{k^\nu_F}{m} + \nu \alpha_R$.
