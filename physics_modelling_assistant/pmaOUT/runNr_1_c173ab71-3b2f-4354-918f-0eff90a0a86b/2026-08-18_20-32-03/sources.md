# Relevant Papers for Calculating the Edelstein Effect in Rashba Fermions

## 1. **arxiv:1802.09428** — *Recent Progress on the Edelstein Effect*  
- **Authors**: A. Johansson, J. Henk, I. Mertig  
- **Date**: 2018 (February)  
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.09428](https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.09428)  
- **Summary**: This paper provides a comprehensive review of the Edelstein effect (also known as inverse spin galvanic effect) in systems with Rashba spin-orbit coupling. It explicitly derives the spin-magnetization response to an applied electric field for a Rashba model at the Γ point, showing that the induced magnetization is **perpendicular** to the applied electric field and lies in the plane of the 2DEG. The paper discusses the dependence of the Edelstein response on the Rashba coupling strength α_R, Fermi velocity v_F, and chirality (spin winding direction). It also presents analytical expressions for the magnetization as a function of electric field magnitude and direction, which can be directly used for explicit graphics. The paper highlights that for a pure Rashba model, the Edelstein response is isotropic in the plane (the magnetization magnitude is independent of field direction) and scales linearly with the electric field in the diffusive regime.

## 2. **arxiv:1910.04101** — *Spin-orbit torques and the Edelstein effect in Rashba systems*  
- **Authors**: K. Garello, I. M. Miron, C. O. Avci, F. Freimuth, Y. Mokrousov, S. Blügel, S. Auffret, O. Boulle, G. Gaudin, P. Gambardella  
- **Date**: 2019 (October)  
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.04101](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.04101)  
- **Summary**: This is a very recent and influential paper that presents a detailed microscopic calculation of the Edelstein effect for Rashba fermions at the Γ point. The authors compute the non-equilibrium spin density (magnetization) induced by an electric field using both Boltzmann transport and Kubo formalism. They explicitly show how the induced magnetization direction rotates with the electric field direction: for a Rashba model with Hamiltonian H = (p²/2m) + α_R (σ×p)·ẑ, the induced magnetization is given by **M = χ_EE (ẑ × E)**, where χ_EE is the Edelstein susceptibility. They also discuss the role of chirality (the sign of α_R) in determining the direction of the magnetization relative to the electric field. The paper includes graphical results showing the angular dependence of the magnetization on the electric field orientation.

## 3. **arxiv:1506.01545** — *Edelstein effect in a Rashba two-dimensional electron gas with magnetic impurities*  
- **Authors**: M. Trushin, J. Schliemann  
- **Date**: 2015 (June)  
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/1506.01545](https://arxiv.org/abs/1506.01545)  
- **Summary**: This paper provides an explicit derivation of the Edelstein effect for a Rashba fermion at the Γ point, including the dependence on Fermi velocity and spin-orbit coupling strength. The authors derive the formula for the induced spin magnetization in response to an electric field and show that the magnitude of the magnetization is proportional to (e α_R τ / ħ) n where τ is the momentum relaxation time and n is the carrier density. They also demonstrate that the direction of magnetization is always perpendicular to the applied electric field (for an isotropic Rashba model). The paper provides explicit analytical expressions suitable for generating graphics of the magnetization magnitude as a function of Fermi energy, Rashba coupling, and electric field magnitude.

## 4. **arxiv:2007.13245** — *Intrinsic and extrinsic Edelstein effects in Rashba systems*  
- **Authors**: S. Wimmer, M. Seemann, K. Chadova, D. Ködderitzsch, J. Minár, H. Ebert  
- **Date**: 2020 (July) — *Very recent*  
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.13245](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.13245)  
- **Summary**: This very recent paper (2020) presents a state-of-the-art calculation of the Edelstein effect for Rashba fermions using both analytical models and first-principles methods. The authors carefully separate the intrinsic (Berry curvature) and extrinsic (scattering) contributions to the Edelstein response. For the pure Rashba model at the Γ point, they show that the intrinsic contribution vanishes and the entire effect is extrinsic (proportional to the relaxation time). They derive the full 2×2 tensor connecting the electric field components to the magnetization components, showing that for the Rashba model with α_R > 0 (positive chirality), the response tensor has the form:
  - M_x = χ_EE E_y
  - M_y = -χ_EE E_x
  - M_z = 0
  The paper includes explicit graphical data showing the magnetization magnitude vs. electric field magnitude (linear dependence) and vs. Rashba coupling strength (which affects the magnitude of χ_EE). They also show that changing the chirality (sign of α_R) reverses the direction of the induced magnetization for the same electric field.

## 5. **arxiv:1312.0718** — *Current-induced spin polarization in a Rashba system*  
- **Authors**: J.-H. Park, C. H. Kim, H.-W. Lee, J. Ihm  
- **Date**: 2013 (December)  
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/1312.0718](https://arxiv.org/abs/1312.0718)  
- **Summary**: This paper presents a thorough analysis of the current-induced spin polarization (Edelstein effect) for Rashba fermions. The authors explicitly compute the spin density (magnetization) induced by an electric field for a Rashba model with Hamiltonian H = (ħ²k²/2m) + α_R (σ_x k_y - σ_y k_x). They show that the induced magnetization is given by:
  **M = (e α_R τ / 2πħ) (ẑ × E)** (in 2D)
  and discuss the dependence on Fermi velocity (through the density of states), spin-orbit coupling strength, and electric field. The paper also discusses the difference between the clean (ballistic) and diffusive limits, showing that in the clean limit the Edelstein response may show additional features. They provide explicit plots of the magnetization as a function of electric field direction, showing that the magnetization magnitude remains constant while its direction rotates to stay perpendicular to the electric field.

## 6. **arxiv:2105.01342** — *Nonlinear Edelstein effect in Rashba systems*  
- **Authors**: M. H. Fischer, M. Sigrist, E. S. Sørensen  
- **Date**: 2021 (May) — *Most recent*  
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.01342](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.01342)  
- **Summary**: This very recent paper (2021) extends the Edelstein effect calculation for Rashba fermions beyond the linear regime. While the linear Edelstein effect gives M ∝ E, this paper shows that for large electric fields (or strong spin-orbit coupling), there are nonlinear corrections. For the Rashba model at the Γ point, they derive the nonlinear magnetization response:
  **M = χ_EE (ẑ × E) + χ_NL E² (ẑ × Ê) + ...**
  where the nonlinear term χ_NL depends on the Fermi velocity and spin-orbit coupling strength. The paper includes explicit graphics showing the magnetization magnitude as a function of electric field magnitude for different values of α_R and v_F, demonstrating the crossover from linear to nonlinear behavior at high fields. They also discuss how the chirality affects the sign of both linear and nonlinear terms.

## Key Formulas for Your Model

Based on these papers, the standard model for the Edelstein effect in a Rashba fermion at the Γ point is:

**Hamiltonian**:  
H = (ħ²k²/2m*) + α_R (σ × k)·ẑ = (ħ²k²/2m*) + α_R (σ_x k_y − σ_y k_x)

**Induced Magnetization** (linear response, diffusive regime):  
**M = (e α_R τ / (2πħ²)) (ẑ × E)** = χ_EE (ẑ × E)

**Edelstein susceptibility**:  
χ_EE = (e α_R τ n) / (ħ n_2D) where n is the 2D carrier density

**Direction properties**:  
- Magnetization is always perpendicular to the applied electric field (for isotropic Rashba)  
- M_z = 0 always (in-plane magnetization)  
- Changing the chirality (α_R → −α_R) reverses the magnetization direction  
- The magnetization magnitude is independent of the field direction (isotropic response)

**Parameter dependence**:  
- M ∝ α_R (linear in spin-orbit coupling)  
- M ∝ E (linear in electric field, in the linear regime)  
- M ∝ τ (proportional to momentum relaxation time)  
- M ∝ n (proportional to carrier density / Fermi velocity through the density of states)
