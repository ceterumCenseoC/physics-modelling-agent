

# Step-by-Step Derivation

The structure factor $S(\vec{K})$ for a crystal with lattice sites $\vec{R}$ and atomic displacement $\vec{u}(\vec{R})$ is defined as:
$$S(\vec{K}) = \sum_{\vec{R}} f(\vec{K}) e^{i \vec{K} \cdot [\vec{R} + \vec{u}(\vec{R})]}$$
where $f(\vec{K})$ is the atomic form factor and $\vec{K}$ is the scattering vector. For a simple cubic lattice with one atom per primitive cell, we can factor out $f$. Given the periodic strain field $\vec{u}(\vec{R}) = \vec{\varepsilon} \sin(\vec{Q} \cdot \vec{R})$ with $|\vec{\varepsilon}| \ll a$, we expand the exponential to first order in $\varepsilon$:
$$e^{i \vec{K} \cdot \vec{u}(\vec{R})} \approx 1 + i \vec{K} \cdot \vec{u}(\vec{R})$$
Substituting this into the structure factor expression yields:
$$S(\vec{K}) \approx f \sum_{\vec{R}} e^{i \vec{K} \cdot \vec{R}} + i f \sum_{\vec{R}} (\vec{K} \cdot \vec{\varepsilon}) \sin(\vec{Q} \cdot \vec{R}) e^{i \vec{K} \cdot \vec{R}}$$
The first term corresponds to the standard Bragg peaks at reciprocal lattice vectors $\vec{G} = \frac{2\pi}{a}(n_x, n_y, n_z)$. We focus on the second term, which represents the strain-induced sidebands. Using the identity $\sin(x) = \frac{e^{ix} - e^{-ix}}{2i}$:
$$S_1(\vec{K}) = \frac{f (\vec{K} \cdot \vec{\varepsilon})}{2} \sum_{\vec{R}} \left[ e^{i(\vec{K} + \vec{Q}) \cdot \vec{R}} - e^{i(\vec{K} - \vec{Q}) \cdot \vec{R}} \right]$$
The lattice sum $\sum_{\vec{R}} e^{i\vec{P} \cdot \vec{R}}$ is non-zero only when $\vec{P}$ equals a reciprocal lattice vector $\vec{G}'$. Therefore, diffraction peaks (sidebands) appear when:
$$\vec{K} + \vec{Q} = \vec{G}' \quad \text{or} \quad \vec{K} - \vec{Q} = \vec{G}' \implies \vec{K} = \vec{G}' \mp \vec{Q}$$
Given $\vec{\varepsilon} \parallel [100]$ and $\vec{Q} \parallel [100]$ with a lattice-wave period of $M a$, we have $\vec{Q} = \left(\frac{2\pi}{M a}, 0, 0\right)$. The scattering vector for the sidebands becomes:
$$\vec{K}_{\pm} = \frac{2\pi}{a}\left(n_x \mp \frac{1}{M}, n_y, n_z\right)$$
Evaluating $S_1$ at these vectors, and noting that $\vec{K} \cdot \vec{\varepsilon} = K_x \varepsilon = \frac{2\pi \varepsilon}{a}(n_x \mp \frac{1}{M})$, the structure factor for the sidebands is:
$$S_{\pm}(\vec{G} \mp \vec{Q}) = \pm \frac{1}{2} f N \left[ \frac{2\pi \varepsilon}{a}\left(n_x \mp \frac{1}{M}\right) \right] = \pm \frac{\pi f N \varepsilon}{a} \left(n_x \mp \frac{1}{M}\right)$$
where $N$ is the total number of unit cells.

# Criteria for Nonvanishing Structure Factor

For the structure factor $S_{\pm}$ to be nonvanishing, the dot product $\vec{K} \cdot \vec{\varepsilon}$ must be non-zero. Since $\vec{\varepsilon} \parallel [100]$, this requires the $x$-component of the scattering vector to be non-zero:
$$K_x \neq 0 \implies n_x \neq 0$$
In the **lowest-possible-order Brillouin Zone**, we consider the primary sidebands generated by the fundamental strain mode ($p=1$). These appear at fractional indices relative to the main Bragg peaks. Restricting to the $[100]$ scattering direction for the lowest-order observable satellites:
$$n_y = 0, \quad n_z = 0, \quad n_x = \pm \frac{1}{M}$$
(Note: The prompt's condition "besides $n_x = M$" refers to the modulation period definition $Q_x = 2\pi/(Ma)$, which fixes the fractional shift.)

# Final Answer

**Criteria for Nonvanishing Structure Factor:**
$n_y = 0$, $n_z = 0$, and $n_x = \pm 1/M$ (for the primary sidebands in the lowest-order zone along the strain propagation direction). Generally, $\vec{K} \cdot \vec{\varepsilon} \neq 0 \Rightarrow n_x \neq 0$.

**Corresponding Structural Factor (first order in $\varepsilon$):**
$$S_{\pm} = \pm \frac{\pi f N \varepsilon}{a} \left( n_x \mp \frac{1}{M} \right) \approx \pm \frac{\pi f N \varepsilon}{a} n_x$$
*(The approximation drops the $1/M$ term as it is of higher order relative to the integer index $n_x$ for standard Bragg peaks, but exact form is retained above for precision.)*
