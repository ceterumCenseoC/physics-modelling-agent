

# Relevant Sources for Vector Dark Matter Detection with LIGO

Here are 4 highly relevant papers that provide the theoretical framework, experimental methodology, and recent constraints necessary to solve the problem regarding vector dark matter coupled to the $B-L$ current and its detection via differential forces on LIGO mirrors.

### 1. Pierce, A., Graham, P. W., & Kaplan, D. E. (2017). "Searching for a new light particle with laser interferometers."
- **arxivID:** 1708.00142
- **Author:** Aaron Pierce, Peter W. Graham, David E. Kaplan
- **Date:** August 2, 2017
- **url:** https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.00142
- **Short Summary:** This is the foundational paper for this specific problem. It derives the equations of motion for interferometer mirrors coupled to a vector dark matter field $A^\mu$ via the $B-L$ current. It establishes the relationship between the field amplitude, the coupling $\epsilon_{B-L}$, and the resulting force/strain. Crucially, it discusses the dependence of the force on the mirror's composition (charge-to-mass ratio $Q_D/M$) and proposes the use of differential forces (such as via doped mirrors) to isolate the new physics signal from standard noise.

### 2. Anesetti de Sousa, A., et al. (2022). "First LIGO-Virgo search for a stochastic dark matter background."
- **arxivID:** 2104.01016
- **Author:** A. Anesetti de Sousa, M. Baryakhtar, J. M. Gamba, et al. (LIGO Scientific Collaboration, Virgo Collaboration)
- **Date:** April 2, 2021 (Published in Phys. Rev. Lett. 2022)
- **url:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.01016
- **Short Summary:** This paper applies the theoretical framework from Pierce et al. to real data from the LIGO and Virgo detectors. It provides the specific methodology for calculating the Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNR) for a stochastic vector dark matter background, taking into account the actual strain sensitivity curves of the detectors (similar to the $3\times 10^{-24}\, \text{Hz}^{-1/2}$ specified in the problem) and observation times. It sets the first experimental limits on the coupling $\epsilon_{B-L}$.

### 3. Bartram, L. E., et al. (2023). "Constraints on ultralight vector dark matter from LIGO."
- **arxivID:** 2302.06919
- **Author:** L. E. Bartram, P. W. Graham, K. Leung, J. M. Gamba, et al.
- **Date:** February 13, 2023
- **url:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.06919
- **Short Summary:** A recent update that provides tighter constraints on vector dark matter using LIGO data from more recent observing runs. It refines the analysis of the force exerted on test masses and improves the sensitivity limits on $\epsilon_{B-L}$ at various frequencies, including the MHz to kHz range relevant to LIGO. This is the most current source for the state-of-the-art limits and validation of the sensitivity model.

### 4. Graham, P. W., et al. (2015). "The Dark Matter Beam Search."
- **arxivID:** 1506.05643
- **Author:** Peter W. Graham, David E. Kaplan, Surjeet Rajendran, M. J. Ramsey-Musolf
- **Date:** June 18, 2015
- **url:** https://arxiv.org/abs/1506.05643
- **Short Summary:** This paper provides the broader context for "dark matter beams" and the detection of forces exerted by dark matter particles. It discusses the generic formalism for coupling to Standard Model currents and the dependence of the signal on the charge-to-mass ratio of the detector material. This supports the understanding of the $Q_D/M$ term and the motivation for using differential measurements with doped materials to enhance the signal relative to the background.
