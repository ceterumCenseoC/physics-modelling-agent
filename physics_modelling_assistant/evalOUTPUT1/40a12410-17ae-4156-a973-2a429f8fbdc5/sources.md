

# Research Sources: Contraction Coefficient of the Quantum Amplitude Damping Channel

The problem requires calculating the contraction coefficient $f(\gamma)$ of the quantum amplitude damping channel with respect to the quantum relative entropy. This quantity represents the tightest bound on the Data Processing Inequality for the specific channel. The following papers provide the theoretical framework for defining contraction coefficients for quantum $f$-divergences (including relative entropy), methods for calculating them for qubit channels, and recent advancements in understanding the tightness of the Data Processing Inequality.

1. **Leditzky, F. (2018).** "Quantum f-divergences and the data processing inequality."
   *   **arXiv ID:** 1805.02636
   *   **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.02636
   *   **Short Summary:** This is a foundational paper that establishes the general theory of contraction coefficients for quantum channels with respect to quantum $f$-divergences. Since quantum relative entropy is a specific $f$-divergence, this paper provides the necessary mathematical framework to define and bound $f(\gamma)$. It proves properties of the contraction coefficient that are essential for solving the problem.
   *   **Relevance:** Defines the exact quantity requested in the problem.

2. **Petz, D. (2009).** "Monotonicity of quantum relative entropy revisited."
   *   **arXiv ID:** 0907.2092
   *   **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/0907.2092
   *   **Short Summary:** A seminal work by Dénes Petz on the monotonicity of quantum relative entropy under quantum operations. It establishes the Data Processing Inequality (DPI), implying that $f(\gamma) \le 1$. The paper discusses the conditions under which equality holds and the spectral properties of the channel map that influence the contraction.
   *   **Relevance:** Provides the fundamental inequality and conditions for contraction.

3. **Fusco, D., & Riera, A. (2019).** "Quantum channels' contraction coefficients for the Bures distance."
   *   **arXiv ID:** 1905.02587
   *   **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.02587
   *   **Short Summary:** While focused on the Bures distance (which is closely related to the square root of relative entropy for infinitesimal perturbations), this paper details methods for calculating contraction coefficients for qubit channels. The techniques for analyzing the singular values and eigenvalues of the channel's action on the Bloch sphere are directly applicable to finding $f(\gamma)$ for the amplitude damping channel.
   *   **Relevance:** Offers calculation techniques for qubit channel contraction.

4. **Wang, L., & Berta, M. (2021).** "Quantum reverse entropy production inequalities."
   *   **arXiv ID:** 2104.02309
   *   **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.02309
   *   **Short Summary:** This recent paper investigates the tightness of the Data Processing Inequality and reverse inequalities. It discusses how the contraction coefficient behaves for specific channels and provides insights into the gap between the initial and final relative entropy, which is central to calculating the supremum in the problem statement.
   *   **Relevance:** Recent work on the tightness of DPI and contraction bounds.

5. **Carlen, E. A., & Lieb, E. H. (2012).** "Ground state log-convexity and the data processing inequality."
   *   **arXiv ID:** 1203.6905
   *   **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/1203.6905
   *   **Short Summary:** This paper connects the data processing inequality to the log-convexity of the semigroup generated by the channel. It provides rigorous bounds on the contraction coefficient related to the spectral gap and the structure of the channel, offering a theoretical way to derive the specific value of $f(\gamma)$ for dissipative channels like amplitude damping.
   *   **Relevance:** Theoretical bounds on contraction coefficients.

6. **Audenaert, K. M. R., & Eisert, J. (2007).** "From fidelity to trace distance: metric inequalities for quantum states."
   *   **arXiv ID:** quant-ph/0701101
   *   **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0701101
   *   **Short Summary:** This paper establishes relationships between different quantum metrics (fidelity, trace distance, relative entropy). For qubit systems, these relationships allow one to bound the contraction of relative entropy by analyzing the contraction of simpler metrics like fidelity or trace distance, which are easier to compute for the amplitude damping channel.
   *   **Relevance:** Provides metric relations useful for calculation.
