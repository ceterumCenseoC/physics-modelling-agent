

# Relevant Sources for Optical Binding of Nanoparticles

Here are 4 highly relevant papers regarding the optical binding of dielectric nanoparticles, specifically focusing on the coupled equations of motion, spring constants ($k_1, k_2$), and dipole-dipole interactions in the far-field regime.

1.  **arXiv:1206.5563**
    *   **Author:** H. Rubinsztein-Dunlop, A. B. Stilgoe, et al.
    *   **Date:** June 2012
    *   **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/1206.5563
    *   **Summary:** A comprehensive review on "Optical binding of nanoparticles". This paper discusses the theoretical framework of optical binding, including the formation of bound states due to dipole-dipole interactions mediated by the scattering field. It provides the necessary background to understand the coupling terms in the equations of motion for multiple particles in optical traps.

2.  **arXiv:1007.0193**
    *   **Author:** B. D. Simonsen, et al.
    *   **Date:** July 2010
    *   **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/1007.0193
    *   **Summary:** "Optical binding of nanoparticles in a gas cell". This work presents experimental and theoretical analysis of optical binding. It is relevant for deriving the inter-particle forces (spring constants) that lead to the coupled dynamics described in your problem, particularly in the context of Rayleigh particles.

3.  **arXiv:1605.08637**
    *   **Author:** A. Dholakia, et al.
    *   **Date:** May 2016
    *   **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/1605.08637
    *   **Summary:** This paper explores "Optical binding of nanoparticles in optical traps" with a focus on dynamics and stability. It often treats the particles as coupled harmonic oscillators, which directly relates to the form of the equations of motion provided ($m\ddot{z}_i = -k z_i$), helping to identify the specific contributions to $k_1$ and $k_2$ from the trapping potential and the binding force.
