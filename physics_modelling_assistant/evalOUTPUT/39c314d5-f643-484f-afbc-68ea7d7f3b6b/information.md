

# Step-by-Step Derivation

To determine the indecomposable gauge-invariant operators in a $U(N)$ gauge theory with an adjoint fermion field $\psi$ carrying global $U(1)$ charge 1, we proceed through the following physical and mathematical steps:

### 1. Construction of Gauge-Invariant Operators
For a field $\psi$ transforming in the adjoint representation of $U(N)$, gauge invariance is achieved by fully contracting gauge indices. The fundamental building blocks of the ring of gauge-invariant operators are single-trace operators formed by taking the trace of products of the field:
$$
\mathcal{O}_k = \text{tr}(\psi^k) = \psi^k_{i_1 i_2} \psi^k_{i_2 i_3} \cdots \psi^k_{i_k i_1}
$$
Each insertion of $\psi$ contributes global $U(1)$ charge $+1$. Thus, an operator containing $k$ fields has total charge $Q = k$. Multi-trace operators can be formed by products of single traces, e.g., $\text{tr}(\psi^a)\text{tr}(\psi^b)$, but these are considered *decomposable* by definition, as they factorize into lower-charge operators [1, 2].

### 2. Algebraic Independence and the Cayley-Hamilton Theorem
For a finite-dimensional gauge group $U(N)$, the number of algebraically independent single-trace generators is finite. Specifically, the ring of polynomial invariants for $N \times N$ matrices is generated by traces of powers up to $N$. Higher powers are constrained by the characteristic polynomial via the **Cayley-Hamilton theorem** [1].

For the rank 2 theory ($N=2$), the $2 \times 2$ matrix $\psi$ satisfies its characteristic equation:
$$
\psi^2 - \text{tr}(\psi)\psi + \frac{1}{2}\left[\text{tr}(\psi)^2 - \text{tr}(\psi^2)\right]\mathbb{I} = 0
$$
Multiplying this equation by $\psi^{k-2}$ and taking the trace yields a recursive relation for higher charges:
$$
\text{tr}(\psi^k) - \text{tr}(\psi)\text{tr}(\psi^{k-1}) + \frac{1}{2}\left[\text{tr}(\psi)^2 - \text{tr}(\psi^2)\right]\text{tr}(\psi^{k-2}) = 0 \quad \text{for } k \geq 2
$$
This relation explicitly demonstrates that $\text{tr}(\psi^k)$ for any $k \geq 3$ can be expressed as a polynomial in $\text{tr}(\psi)$ and $\text{tr}(\psi^2)$. Consequently, all single-trace operators with charge $Q \geq 3$ are **decomposable**.

### 3. Enumeration for Charges $Q \leq 5$
We systematically evaluate operators up to charge 5:
- **Charge 1:** $\text{tr}(\psi)$ is a fundamental generator. It cannot be formed from lower-charge operators. $\Rightarrow$ **Indecomposable**
- **Charge 2:** $\text{tr}(\psi^2)$ is a fundamental generator. It is not a polynomial in $\text{tr}(\psi)$. $\Rightarrow$ **Indecomposable**
- **Charge 3:** Using the $k=3$ case of the recursion: $\text{tr}(\psi^3) = \frac{3}{2}\text{tr}(\psi)\text{tr}(\psi^2) - \frac{1}{2}\text{tr}(\psi)^3$. This is a product of lower-charge generators. $\Rightarrow$ Decomposable
- **Charge 4:** $\text{tr}(\psi^4)$ reduces to a polynomial in $\text{tr}(\psi)$ and $\text{tr}(\psi^2)$ via the $k=4$ recursion. $\Rightarrow$ Decomposable
- **Charge 5:** $\text{tr}(\psi^5)$ reduces similarly via the $k=5$ recursion. $\Rightarrow$ Decomposable

Multi-trace operators (e.g., $\text{tr}(\psi)\text{tr}(\psi)$, $\text{tr}(\psi)\text{tr}(\psi^2)$) are products of single traces and are therefore decomposable by the standard combinatorial definition of gauge-invariant operator counting [1, 2].

Thus, the complete set of indecomposable gauge-invariant operators with charge $Q \leq 5$ in the $U(2)$ theory consists solely of the first two single-trace generators.

# Final Answer
The indecomposable gauge-invariant operators in the rank 2 ($U(2)$) theory with global $U(1)$ charge $Q \leq 5$ are:

1. $\text{tr}(\psi)$  (Charge $Q=1$)
2. $\text{tr}(\psi^2)$ (Charge $Q=2$)

All operators with charge $Q \geq 3$ are decomposable, being expressible as polynomials or products of the above generators due to the $N=2$ Cayley-Hamilton constraints [1, 2].
