# Important Sources

1. **arXiv:quant-ph/0410133**
   - **Author**: M. Horodecki, J. Oppenheim, A. Winter
   - **Date**: 2004-10-08 (Published in Phys. Rev. Lett. 2005)
   - **URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0410133
   - **Short Summary**: Introduces the concept of "privacy without purification" and defines the **private state** (specifically the canonical private state studied here). It proves that these states can be bound entangled (PPT), which implies zero distillable entanglement and, consequently, zero quantum capacity for the associated channel.

2. **arXiv:2407.21701**
   - **Author**: Luís Bugalho, Majid Hassani, Yasser Omar, Damian Markham
   - **Date**: 2024-07-31
   - **URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.21701
   - **Short Summary**: A very recent paper discussing "Private and Robust States for Distributed Quantum Sensing." It utilizes the structure of private states (like the one in the problem) and references their entanglement properties, providing modern context and applications for the class of states defined by Horodecki et al.

3. **arXiv:1007.2855**
   - **Author**: Graeme Smith
   - **Date**: 2010-07-16
   - **URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/1007.2855
   - **Short Summary**: A comprehensive review titled "Quantum Channel Capacities." It details the definitions and relationships between various capacities (classical, private, quantum) and discusses channels with specific structural properties, including those related to entanglement distillation and PPT channels.

4. **arXiv:1105.1040**
   - **Author**: M. E. Shirokov
   - **Date**: 2011-05-05
   - **URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/1105.1040
   - **Short Summary**: Discusses conditions for equality between entanglement-assisted and unassisted capacities. While focused on classical capacities, it provides theoretical bounds and conditions relevant to understanding the limits of channels defined by specific quantum states like Werner and private states.
