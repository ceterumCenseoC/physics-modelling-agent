

# Step-by-Step Derivation

### 1. Conservation Laws in High-Harmonic Generation
In high-harmonic generation (HHG), the interaction between an intense laser field and a gas target is a highly nonlinear, $q$-photon process that produces radiation at the $q$-th harmonic frequency $\omega_q = q\omega_0$. Angular momentum conservation strictly governs the spin (SAM) and orbital (OAM) properties of the emitted harmonic photons relative to the absorbed driving photons.

According to established theory and experimental verification in structured-light HHG [1, 2]:
- **Orbital Angular Momentum (OAM) Conservation:** The total OAM of the emitted harmonic photon equals the sum of the OAMs of the $q$ absorbed fundamental photons. For a driving field with a well-defined topological charge $\ell$, the $q$-th harmonic acquires an OAM given by:
  $$ \ell_q = q \cdot \ell $$
- **Spin Angular Momentum (Helicity) Conservation:** The SAM (helicity $\sigma = \pm 1$) follows a similar summation rule. However, in centrosymmetric media (like noble gas jets), selection rules based on the inversion symmetry of the medium dictate that **only odd harmonics are efficiently generated** for circularly polarized driving fields. For an odd harmonic order $q$, the helicity of the emitted photon is conserved relative to the fundamental:
  $$ \sigma_q = q \cdot \sigma_{\text{fund}} \pmod 2 \implies \sigma_q = \sigma_{\text{fund}} \quad (\text{for odd } q) $$
  *(Note: We adopt the standard physics convention where Left Circular Polarization (LCP) corresponds to helicity $\sigma = +1$ and Right Circular Polarization (RCP) corresponds to $\sigma = -1$.)*

### 2. Application to the Pulse Train
The driving field consists of three temporally distinct pulses with peaks at $t = 0$ fs, $t = 30$ fs, and $t = 60$ fs. Although they are spatially overlapped in the gas jet, their temporal separation (30 fs delay vs. 50 fs FWHM) means HHG occurs sequentially at each pulse peak. The generated 23rd harmonic ($q=23$, which is odd) will therefore be a corresponding train of three pulses. We calculate the OAM and helicity for each harmonic pulse generated by its respective driving pulse.

**Pulse 1 ($t = 0$ fs):**
- Driving properties: $\ell_1 = -1$, $\sigma_1 = +1$ (Left)
- Harmonic OAM: $\ell_{23}^{(1)} = 23 \times (-1) = -23$
- Harmonic Helicity: $\sigma_{23}^{(1)} = +1$

**Pulse 2 ($t = 30$ fs):**
- Driving properties: $\ell_2 = 2$, $\sigma_2 = -1$ (Right)
- Harmonic OAM: $\ell_{23}^{(2)} = 23 \times 2 = 46$
- Harmonic Helicity: $\sigma_{23}^{(2)} = -1$

**Pulse 3 ($t = 60$ fs):**
- Driving properties: $\ell_3 = 1$, $\sigma_3 = +1$ (Left)
- Harmonic OAM: $\ell_{23}^{(3)} = 23 \times 1 = 23$
- Harmonic Helicity: $\sigma_{23}^{(3)} = +1$

### 3. Synthesis of Results
Because HHG is an instantaneous nonlinear process, the macroscopic harmonic field preserves the temporal structure of the driving field. The 23rd harmonic emerges as a pulse train where each harmonic pulse carries the OAM and helicity scaled/conserved from its corresponding fundamental pulse. If spatial interference or modal mixing is considered due to the 30 fs temporal overlap, the field represents a coherent superposition of these three structured components. However, the quantized angular momentum values associated with each generation channel remain strictly defined by the conservation laws above.
