

Here are the most relevant papers for modeling the stochastic bacterial cell size control and population growth dynamics described in your problem:

1. **arxivID**: 2411.08512  
**Author**: Arthur Genthon  
**Date**: 2024-11-13  
**URL**: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.08512v1  
**Short Summary**: Directly addresses how single-cell variability in growth rates, birth/division sizes, and size control strategies impact the overall population fitness (growth rate $\Lambda$). Provides theoretical frameworks connecting division noise and size regulation to asymptotic population growth, making it highly relevant for deriving $\Lambda$ to first order in noise variance.

2. **arxivID**: 2401.05193  
**Author**: Arthur Genthon, Philipp Thomas  
**Date**: 2024-01-12  
**URL**: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.05193v1  
**Short Summary**: Presents stochastic maps that unify phenomenological models of cell size regulation by integrating fluctuating single-cell growth rates and size-dependent noise mechanisms. Explores how extrinsic noise and division precision affect population-level dynamics and growth rate optimization.

3. **arxivID**: 1611.07989  
**Author**: Jie Lin, Ariel Amir  
**Date**: 2016-11-23  
**URL**: https://arxiv.org/pdf/1611.07989v2  
**Short Summary**: Establishes a quantitative link between population growth rate and single-cell generation times under stochastic growth and size control. Derives exact relations showing how mother-daughter correlations and division noise modulate the asymptotic population growth rate, providing foundational equations for perturbative expansions in $\sigma^2$.

4. **arxivID**: 1809.10217  
**Author**: Farshid Jafarpour  
**Date**: 2018-09-26  
**URL**: https://arxiv.org/pdf/1809.10217v2  
**Short Summary**: Analyzes how cell-size regulation induces dynamics in population growth rates. Demonstrates mathematically how non-zero correlations from size control rules (parameterized by $\beta$) affect the approach to steady-state growth and the value of the asymptotic rate $\Lambda$.
