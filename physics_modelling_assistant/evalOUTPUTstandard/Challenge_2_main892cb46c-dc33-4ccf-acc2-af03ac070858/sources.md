

# Relevant Sources for Cell Size Control & Population Growth Rate Modeling

1. **arxivID:** 1611.07989
   **Author:** Jie Lin, Ariel Amir
   **Date:** November 2016
   **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.07989
   **Short Summary:** This foundational paper establishes the exact quantitative link between single-cell generation time fluctuations, cell-size control strategies, and the asymptotic population growth rate $\Lambda$. It derives how the size-regulation parameter $\beta$ and division noise variance $\sigma^2$ modulate population fitness, providing the core mathematical framework needed to solve the main problem.

2. **arxivID:** 2411.08512
   **Author:** Arthur Genthon
   **Date:** November 2024
   **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.08512
   **Short Summary:** A very recent study that directly investigates how noisy cell-size control impacts population growth. It analyzes how variability in birth/division sizes and growth rate fluctuations determine the long-term population fitness, offering modern theoretical insights into the first-order dependence of $\Lambda$ on $\sigma^2$ and $\beta$.

3. **arxivID:** 1809.10217
   **Author:** Farshid Jafarpour
   **Date:** September 2018
   **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/1809.10217
   **Short Summary:** Explores how cell-size regulation induces mother-daughter correlations in generation times and sustains oscillations in population growth. The mathematical treatment of size-dependent division thresholds and stochastic growth rates is highly relevant for deriving the asymptotic growth rate under size control.

4. **arxivID:** 2209.14683
   **Author:** Yaïr Hein, Farshid Jafarpour
   **Date:** September 2022
   **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.14683
   **Short Summary:** Examines the asymptotic behavior of population growth rate and cell size distributions when single-cell growth rates fluctuate stochastically. It provides key analytical tools for connecting time-varying growth rates (like the two-state process $\lambda_t$) to the macroscopic exponential growth rate $\Lambda$.

5. **arxivID:** 2401.05193
   **Author:** Arthur Genthon, Philipp Thomas
   **Date:** January 2024
   **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.05193
   **Short Summary:** Introduces unified stochastic maps that integrate fluctuating single-cell growth rates with size-dependent noise mechanisms and cell-size control. This framework is directly applicable to modeling the interplay between $\lambda_t$, division size regulation ($\beta$), and noise ($\xi$) to predict population dynamics.
