

# Relevant Sources

1.  **Does high harmonic generation conserve angular momentum?**
    *   **arxivID:** 1310.1206
    *   **Author:** Avner Fleischer, Ofer Kfir, Tzvi Diskin, Pavel Sidorenko, Oren Cohen
    *   **date:** 2013-10-04
    *   **url:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/1310.1206v1
    *   **short summary:** This paper establishes the fundamental conservation laws for orbital angular momentum (OAM) and spin angular momentum (SAM) in high-harmonic generation. It proves that the N-th harmonic conserves the sum of the angular momenta of the N photons absorbed from the driving field. This provides the core theoretical framework ($\ell_N = \sum \ell_i$, $\sigma_N = \sum \sigma_i$) required to calculate the OAM and helicity of the 23rd harmonic from the driving pulses.

2.  **Conservation of torus-knot angular momentum in high-order harmonic generation**
    *   **arxivID:** 1810.06503
    *   **Author:** Emilio Pisanty, Laura Rego, Julio San Román, Antonio Picón, Kevin M. Dorney, Henry C. Kapteyn, Margaret M. Murnane, Luis Plaja, Maciej Lewenstein, Carlos Hernández-García
    *   **date:** 2018-10-15
    *   **url:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/1810.06503v2
    *   **short summary:** This study extends the conservation laws to complex vectorial driving fields (torus-knot beams). It confirms that OAM and helicity are conserved even for structured light with complex spatiotemporal profiles, which is directly relevant for modeling the high-harmonic generation driven by the superposition of three distinct structured pulses in your setup.

3.  **Controlling Photon Transverse Orbital Angular Momentum in High Harmonic Generation**
    *   **arxivID:** 2308.05398
    *   **Author:** Yiqi Fang, Shengyue Lu, Yunquan Liu
    *   **date:** 2023-08-10
    *   **url:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.05398v1
    *   **short summary:** This recent paper investigates high-harmonic generation using spatiotemporal optical vortex (STOV) pulses, focusing on the generation of transverse OAM. It provides the most current insights into how structured driving fields dictate the angular momentum properties of the generated EUV light, serving as a modern reference for structured light control in HHG.
