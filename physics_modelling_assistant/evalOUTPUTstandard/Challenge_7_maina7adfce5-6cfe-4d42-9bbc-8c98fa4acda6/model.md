# Mathematical Model for Quantum Fisher Information in Noisy Distributed Sensing

## Model Definition
This model calculates the Quantum Fisher Information (QFI) for estimating the scaled average parameter $\theta_1 = \frac{1}{\sqrt{d}} \sum_{i=1}^d x_i$ in a distributed quantum sensor network subject to dephasing noise. The model accounts for:
1. A distributed noisy GHZ state probe.
2. Independent single-qubit dephasing dynamics.
3. An accumulation of local phases $x_i = \omega^{(i)} t$.

## Step-by-Step Derivation

### Step 1: Characterizing the Initial Noisy State
The initial probe state is a depolarized $nd$-qubit GHZ state distributed across $d$ nodes. Based on the problem setup, the GHZ state has a fidelity $F(n) = F k^{n-1}$.

A depolarized GHZ state can be viewed as a mixture of the ideal GHZ state (with coherence amplitude $1$) and a maximally mixed state (with coherence amplitude $0$). For QFI calculations involving parameter estimation via the accumulated phase, the relevant figure of merit is the effective coherence of the state, which scales directly with the fidelity[1].

Thus, the effective coherence amplitude of the initial state is $A_{initial} = F(n) = F k^{n-1}$.

### Step 2: Modeling the Noisy Sensing Dynamics
The dynamics of the $nd$ sensor qubits are governed by the Lindblad master equation:
$$ \frac{d}{dt}\rho = -i\frac{\omega^{(i)}}{2}\left[\sigma_z^{(i,k)},\rho\right] + \frac{\gamma}{2}\left(\sigma_z^{(i,k)}\rho \sigma_z^{(i,k)} - \rho\right) $$
This equation describes unitary precession at frequency $\omega^{(i)}$ combined with phase damping (dephasing) at rate $\gamma$.

For a parameter-dependent evolution of this form on an interferometric probe state, the effect of the noise is to suppress the off-diagonal elements (coherences) of the density matrix in the energy eigenbasis. Since the noise acts independently on each of the $nd$ qubits, the collective coherence term $\rho_{0,N}$ (the amplitude of the maximally coherent component) decays multiplicatively [1].

The effective coherence amplitude after time $t$ is given by the product of the initial amplitude and the decay factor:
$$ \lambda_{eff}(t) = A_{initial} \cdot e^{-nd \gamma t} = F k^{n-1} e^{-nd \gamma t} $$

### Step 3: Quantum Fisher Information (QFI) for the Distributed Network
The sensing Hamiltonian is a sum of local $\sigma_z$ operators on the qubits. We seek to estimate a linear combination of the local parameters $\theta_1 = \sum_{i=1}^d x_i / \sqrt{d}$, where $x_i = \omega^{(i)} t$.

According to the theory of quantum metrology in distributed networks under dephasing noise [1], the QFI matrix element for estimating a parameter generated by such local operators is proportional to the square of the effective coherence and the square of the number of qubits contributing to the phase shift at the relevant nodes.

For a state distributed over $d$ nodes with $n$ qubits per node, the QFI for the scaled average $\theta_1$ is derived from the generalized variance associated with the parameter vector $\vec{x}$:
$$ \mathcal{F}(\theta_1) = \mathbf{c}^T Q \mathbf{c} $$
where $\mathbf{c} = (1/\sqrt{d}, \dots, 1/\sqrt{d})$ is the coefficient vector defining $\theta_1$, and $Q$ is the QFI matrix for the original parameters $\vec{x}$.

For the GHZ structure described, the matrix elements $Q_{ij}$ are [1]:
$$ Q_{ij} = 4 \frac{(\Delta \lambda)^2}{\sum \lambda} n^2 $$
Here, $\Delta \lambda = \lambda_{eff}(t)$ is the difference in eigenvalues corresponding to the coherent subspace (essentially the coherence amplitude), and $\sum \lambda$ is the total population of that subspace. Assuming the "coherent" subspace (GHZ sector) is normalized (i.e., $\sum \lambda = 1$), this simplifies significantly.

Substituting the coefficients $c_i = 1/\sqrt{d}$:
$$ \mathcal{F}(\theta_1) = \sum_{i,j} \frac{1}{\sqrt{d}} \frac{1}{\sqrt{d}} \left( 4 \lambda_{eff}(t)^2 n^2 \right) $$
$$ \mathcal{F}(\theta_1) = \frac{1}{d} \left( \sum_{i,j} 1_{ij} \right) 4 \lambda_{eff}(t)^2 n^2 $$
$$ \mathcal{F}(\theta_1) = \frac{1}{d} (d^2) 4 \lambda_{eff}(t)^2 n^2 $$
$$ \mathcal{F}(\theta_1) = 4 d n^2 \lambda_{eff}(t)^2 $$

### Step 4: Substitution and Final Expression
We substitute the effective coherence derived in Step 2 into the QFI expression from Step 3.

$$ \mathcal{F}(\theta_1) = 4 d n^2 \left( F k^{n-1} e^{-nd \gamma t} \right)^2 $$
$$ \mathcal{F}(\theta_1) = 4 d n^2 F^2 k^{2n-2} \left( e^{-nd \gamma t} \right)^2 $$
$$ \mathcal{F}(\theta_1) = 4 d n^2 F^2 k^{2n-2} e^{-2nd \gamma t} $$

Finally, we use the provided variable $q = \frac{1 + e^{-\gamma t}}{2}$. Solving for the exponential term:
$$ 2q = 1 + e^{-\gamma t} \implies e^{-\gamma t} = 2q - 1 $$

Substituting $(2q - 1)^{nd}$ for $e^{-nd \gamma t}$:

$$ \mathcal{F}(\theta_1) = 4 d n^2 F^2 k^{2n-2} (2q - 1)^{2nd} $$

This is the Quantum Fisher Information for the parameter $\theta_1$ in terms of the number of nodes $d$, qubits per node $n$, base fidelity $F$, exponential decay factor $k$, and the decoherence variable $q$.
