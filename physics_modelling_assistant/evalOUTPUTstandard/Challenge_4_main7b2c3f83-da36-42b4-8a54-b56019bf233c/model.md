To determine the Orbital Angular Momentum (OAM) and helicity of the 23rd harmonic order generated by the structured three-pulse laser field, we must construct a mathematical model based on the conservation laws of angular momentum in the strong-field regime. High-harmonic generation (HHG) is a non-perturbative process, but for the purposes of determining the **selection rules** (the allowed values of OAM and helicity), we can rely on the conservation of the "Torus-Knot Angular Momentum" (TKAM) or the simpler conservation of OAM and Spin Angular Momentum (SAM) separately.

### **1. Mathematical Definition of the Driving Field**

The problem describes a driving electric field $E(\mathbf{r}, t)$ composed of three pulses. We assume the pulses are co-propagating along the $z$-axis and overlapped spatially.

Let the electric field of the $j$-th pulse ($j=1, 2, 3$) be defined in cylindrical coordinates $(\rho, \phi, z)$. The field for a pulse with center wavelength $\lambda_0$ (angular frequency $\omega_0 = 2\pi c/\lambda_0$), pulse duration $\tau$ (FWHM), peak time $t_j$, topological charge $\ell_j$, and helicity $\sigma_j$ is given by:

$$ \mathbf{E}_j(\rho, \phi, z, t) = \mathcal{E}_0 f(t - t_j) \mathcal{G}(\rho, z) e^{i(\ell_j \phi + \sigma_j \omega_0 t - k_0 z)} \mathbf{e}_{\sigma_j} + \text{c.c.} $$

Where:
*   $\mathcal{E}_0$ is the scalar amplitude.
*   $f(t - t_j)$ is the temporal envelope (e.g., a Gaussian). Given a FWHM of 50 fs, the intensity envelope is $I(t) \propto \exp(-4 \ln 2 (t - t_j)^2 / \tau^2)$.
*   $\mathcal{G}(\rho, z)$ represents the spatial beam profile.
*   $k_0$ is the wavenumber.
*   $\mathbf{e}_{\sigma_j}$ is the polarization vector: $\mathbf{e}_{+1} = (\hat{x} + i\hat{y})/\sqrt{2}$ for Left Circular Polarization (LCP) and $\mathbf{e}_{-1} = (\hat{x} - i\hat{y})/\sqrt{2}$ for Right Circular Polarization (RCP).
*   $\sigma_j$ is the helicity: $\sigma = +1$ for LCP and $\sigma = -1$ for RCP.

### **2. Determination of Interaction Mechanism**

The crucial step in the model is to determine whether the pulses interact **simultaneously** (multicolor generation) or **sequentially** (independent generation summed in time).

*   **Timing:** The peaks are at $t = 0$ fs, $t = 30$ fs, and $t = 60$ fs.
*   **Duration:** The pulses have a FWHM duration of 50 fs.
*   **Separation:** The separation between peaks is 30 fs.

The standard deviation of a Gaussian with FWHM $\tau=50$ fs is $\sigma_t = 50 / (2\sqrt{2\ln 2}) \approx 21$ fs.
Since the time separation ($30$ fs) is greater than the standard deviation but less than the FWHM, the pulses have **partial temporal overlap**.

However, the fundamental physical mechanism of HHG can be modeled using the **Three-Step Model**: ionization, acceleration, and recombination. This process is deterministic and driven by the instantaneous laser field. The emission of the $q$-th harmonic at the central frequency $q\omega_0$ is primarily generated by the peak of the pulses where the intensity is highest.

Because the center frequencies of all three pulses are identical ($800$ nm), there is no frequency mixing or "color" logic (like $q \omega_1 + p \omega_2$). All three pulses contribute to the same harmonic orders.

The most robust model for this setup treats the process as a **superposition of three single-color HHG events**. The conservation laws apply to the interaction of the electron wavepacket with the specific field configuration active during its trajectory. Since the central peaks are distinct, we treat the dominant contribution of each pulse to the harmonic signal as originating from its respective peak time.

### **3. Angular Momentum Conservation Model**

#### **3.1 Helicity Conservation**

Spin angular momentum (helicity) is conserved during the recollision process in dipole approximation. The harmonic field $H_q$ generated by a driver with helicity $\sigma$ will retain that helicity:
$$ \sigma_q = \sigma_{\text{driver}} $$
This is a well-known empirical selection rule: LCP light drives LCP harmonics, and RCP light drives RCP harmonics.

#### **3.2 OAM Conservation**

For single-color HHG where the driving field carries a well-defined OAM $\ell$, the OAM of the $q$-th harmonic, $\ell_q$, scales linearly with the harmonic order. This is derived from the phase-matching condition in the paraxial approximation and conservation of total angular momentum.

As stated by Hernández-García et al. and Pisanty et al.:
$$ \ell_q = q \cdot \ell_{\text{driver}} $$
The "extra" OAM $q\ell$ is interpreted as arising from the $q$-photon absorption nature of the process, where each absorbed photon contributes $\ell$ units of OAM.

### **4. Application to the 23rd Harmonic**

We calculate the OAM and helicity contribution from each of the three pulses for the $q=23$ harmonic.

**Parameters:**
*   $q = 23$
*   Pulse 1: $\ell_1 = -1$, $\sigma_1 = +1$ (LCP)
*   Pulse 2: $\ell_2 = 2$, $\sigma_2 = -1$ (RCP)
*   Pulse 3: $\ell_3 = 1$, $\sigma_3 = +1$ (LCP)

**Calculations:**

**Contribution from Pulse 1 ($t=0$):**
$$ \ell_{23}^{(1)} = q \cdot \ell_1 = 23 \cdot (-1) = -23 $$
$$ \sigma_{23}^{(1)} = \sigma_1 = +1 $$

**Contribution from Pulse 2 ($t=30 \text{ fs}$):**
$$ \ell_{23}^{(2)} = q \cdot \ell_2 = 23 \cdot 2 = 46 $$
$$ \sigma_{23}^{(2)} = \sigma_2 = -1 $$

**Contribution from Pulse 3 ($t=60 \text{ fs}$):**
$$ \ell_{23}^{(3)} = q \cdot \ell_3 = 23 \cdot 1 = 23 $$
$$ \sigma_{23}^{(3)} = \sigma_3 = +1 $$

### **5. Final Model Output**

The model describes the generated 23rd harmonic field $E_{23}(\rho, \phi, z, t)$ not as a single vortex, but as a **time-dependent superposition of three distinct vortex modes**.

The total electric field of the 23rd harmonic is:
$$ \mathbf{E}_{23}(\rho, \phi, z, t) = \sum_{j=1}^{3} A_j(t - t_j) \exp\left(i \ell_{23}^{(j)} \phi\right) \mathbf{e}_{\sigma_{23}^{(j)}} $$

Where $A_j(t - t_j)$ represents the temporal envelope of the harmonic emission from the $j$-th pulse, centered at $t_j$.

**Summary of Results for the 23rd Harmonic:**

The 23rd harmonic consists of three components associated with the three driving pulses in time:

1.  **At $t \approx 0$ fs:**
    *   **OAM:** $-23$
    *   **Helicity ($\sigma$):** $+1$ (Left Circularly Polarized)

2.  **At $t \approx 30$ fs:**
    *   **OAM:** $+46$
    *   **Helicity ($\sigma$):** $-1$ (Right Circularly Polarized)

3.  **At $t \approx 60$ fs:**
    *   **OAM:** $+23$
    *   **Helicity ($\sigma$):** $+1$ (Left Circularly Polarized)

Because these components have different OAM values ($\ell = -23, +46, +23$) and different helicities, they are orthogonal modes. If measured without temporal resolution (integrated over all time), the signal will be the incoherent sum of the intensities of these three modes. It does not converge to a single OAM or helicity value but rather creates a time-varying structured EUV field.
