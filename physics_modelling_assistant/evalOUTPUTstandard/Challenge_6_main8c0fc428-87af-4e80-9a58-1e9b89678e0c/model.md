# Mathematical Model for Twisted Bilayer MoTe$_2$ Topological Properties

## 1. Model Parameters and Geometric Setup

We first define the geometric parameters and vectors necessary to construct the Hamiltonian for twisted bilayer MoTe$_2$ at the K valley.

**1.1 Physical Constants and Model Parameters**

Given the monolayer lattice constant $a_0 = 3.52 \text{ \AA}$ and the twist angle $\theta = 3.5^\circ$, the moiré lattice period $a_M$ is calculated as:
$$
a_M = \frac{a_0}{2 \sin\left( \frac{\theta}{2} \right)} = \frac{3.52}{2 \sin(1.75^\circ)} \approx 57.78 \text{ \AA}
$$

The effective kinetic energy coefficient is derived from the given constants:
$$
\frac{\hbar^2}{2m^*} = \frac{\hbar^2}{2(0.6 m_e)} = \frac{1}{0.6} \left( \frac{\hbar}{2 m_e} \right) \times 2 \hbar m_e = \frac{2}{0.6} \times 7619.96423 \approx 25399.88 \text{ meV} \cdot \text{\AA}^2
$$

The Hamiltonian parameters are:
*   Moiré potential amplitude: $V = 16.5$ meV
*   Interlayer tunneling: $w = -18.8$ meV
*   Potential phase: $\psi = -105.9^\circ$

**1.2 Reciprocal Lattice Vectors**

The base moiré reciprocal lattice vector $\boldsymbol{g}_1$ and its magnitude $G = |\boldsymbol{g}_1|$ are:
$$
\boldsymbol{g}_1 = \frac{4 \pi}{\sqrt{3} a_M} \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix} \approx \frac{4 \pi}{\sqrt{3} (57.78)} \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix} \approx 0.126 \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix} \text{ \AA}^{-1}
$$
$$
G = |\boldsymbol{g}_1| \approx 0.126 \text{ \AA}^{-1}
$$

The three reciprocal lattice vectors $\boldsymbol{g}_i$ are generated by $120^\circ$ rotations ($C_3$):
$$
\boldsymbol{g}_i = C_3^{i-1} \boldsymbol{g}_1, \quad i=1,2,3
$$
Explicitly:
$$
\boldsymbol{g}_1 = G \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix}, \quad \boldsymbol{g}_2 = G \begin{pmatrix} -1/2 \\ \sqrt{3}/2 \end{pmatrix}, \quad \boldsymbol{g}_3 = G \begin{pmatrix} -1/2 \\ -\sqrt{3}/2 \end{pmatrix}
$$

The basis vectors for the Brillouin zone sampling are defined as:
$$
\boldsymbol{b}_1 = \boldsymbol{g}_1, \quad \boldsymbol{b}_2 = \boldsymbol{g}_1 + \boldsymbol{g}_2
$$

**1.3 Interlayer Coupling Vectors**

The base interlayer coupling vector $\boldsymbol{q}_1$ and its family are defined as:
$$
\boldsymbol{q}_1 = G \begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ 1/\sqrt{3} \end{pmatrix}, \quad \boldsymbol{q}_i = C_3^{i-1} \boldsymbol{q}_1
$$

## 2. Discretized Momentum-Space Hamiltonian

To solve the problem numerically, we convert the continuum real-space Hamiltonian into a matrix Hamiltonian in the momentum space basis using the specified Fourier conventions [1,2].

**2.1 Definition of the Basis Set**

We define a set of plane wave vectors $\boldsymbol{Q}$ associated with the top layer ($t$) and bottom layer ($b$).

*   **Top Layer ($\boldsymbol{Q}_t$):** The set of vectors satisfying:
    1.  $\boldsymbol{Q}_t - \boldsymbol{q}_1$ is a reciprocal lattice vector (i.e., $\boldsymbol{Q}_t = \boldsymbol{q}_1 + \sum_{i=1}^3 n_i \boldsymbol{g}_i$ for integers $n_i$).
    2.  The cutoff condition: $|\boldsymbol{Q}_t| < 4.1 |\boldsymbol{b}_1| = 4.1 G$.

*   **Bottom Layer ($\boldsymbol{Q}_b$):** The set of vectors satisfying:
    1.  $\boldsymbol{Q}_b + \boldsymbol{q}_1$ is a reciprocal lattice vector (i.e., $\boldsymbol{Q}_b = -\boldsymbol{q}_1 + \sum_{i=1}^3 n_i \boldsymbol{g}_i$).
    2.  The cutoff condition: $|\boldsymbol{Q}_b| < 4.1 G$.

The total Hamiltonian matrix at crystal momentum $\boldsymbol{k}$ is constructed in the basis of layer-pseudospin and plane wave indices. The basis states are of the form $| \boldsymbol{k}-\boldsymbol{Q}, l \rangle$ where $l \in \{b, t\}$.

**2.2 Matrix Elements of the Hamiltonian**

For a fixed crystal momentum $\boldsymbol{k}$ in the Brillouin zone, the Hamiltonian matrix $\mathcal{H}(\boldsymbol{k})$ has the following block structure:

$$
\mathcal{H}(\boldsymbol{k}) = \begin{pmatrix} \mathcal{H}_{bb} & \mathcal{H}_{bt} \\ \mathcal{H}_{tb} & \mathcal{H}_{tt} \end{pmatrix}
$$

**Kinetic Energy Terms:**
The kinetic energy is diagonal and identical for both layers:
$$
\langle \boldsymbol{k}-\boldsymbol{Q}, l | \mathcal{H}_{kin} | \boldsymbol{k}-\boldsymbol{Q}', l' \rangle = \delta_{ll'} \delta_{\boldsymbol{Q}, \boldsymbol{Q}'} \frac{\hbar^2}{2m^*} |\boldsymbol{k}-\boldsymbol{Q}|^2
$$

**Intralayer Potential Terms:**
The periodic potential $V(\boldsymbol{r})$ scattering electrons within the same layer connects states differing by a reciprocal lattice vector $\boldsymbol{g}_i$.
$$
\langle \boldsymbol{k}-\boldsymbol{Q}, l | \mathcal{H}_{pot} | \boldsymbol{k}-\boldsymbol{Q}', l' \rangle = \delta_{ll'} V_{ll}(\boldsymbol{Q}' - \boldsymbol{Q})
$$
where the matrix elements are derived from $2 V \sum_{i=1}^3 \cos(\boldsymbol{g}_i \cdot \boldsymbol{r} \mp \psi)$:
$$
V_{tt}(\boldsymbol{G}) = \begin{cases} V e^{-i \psi} & \text{if } \boldsymbol{G} = \boldsymbol{g}_i \\ V e^{i \psi} & \text{if } \boldsymbol{G} = -\boldsymbol{g}_i \\ 0 & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}
$$
$$
V_{bb}(\boldsymbol{G}) = \begin{cases} V e^{-i (-\psi)} = V e^{i\psi} & \text{if } \boldsymbol{G} = \boldsymbol{g}_i \\ V e^{i (-\psi)} = V e^{-i\psi} & \text{if } \boldsymbol{G} = -\boldsymbol{g}_i \\ 0 & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}
$$

**Interlayer Tunneling Terms:**
The interlayer tunneling $T(\boldsymbol{r})$ connects the bottom and top layers with momentum transfer $\pm \boldsymbol{q}_i$.
$$
\langle \boldsymbol{k}-\boldsymbol{Q}_b, b | \mathcal{H}_{tun} | \boldsymbol{k}-\boldsymbol{Q}_t, t \rangle = w \sum_{i=1}^3 \delta_{\boldsymbol{Q}_t - \boldsymbol{Q}_b, \boldsymbol{q}_i}
$$
$$
\langle \boldsymbol{k}-\boldsymbol{Q}_t, t | \mathcal{H}_{tun} | \boldsymbol{k}-\boldsymbol{Q}_b, b \rangle = w^* \sum_{i=1}^3 \delta_{\boldsymbol{Q}_b - \boldsymbol{Q}_t, -\boldsymbol{q}_i}
$$

**2.3 Brillouin Zone Integration Grid**

The set of crystal momenta $\boldsymbol{k}$ used for numerical evaluation is the uniform grid $\mathcal{K}$:
$$
\mathcal{K} = \left\{ \left( \frac{l_1}{L} - \frac{1}{2} \right) \boldsymbol{b}_1 + \left( \frac{l_2}{L} - \frac{1}{2} \right) \boldsymbol{b}_2 \mid l_1, l_2 = 0, \dots, L-1 \right\}
$$
with $L=60$. This provides $N_k = L \times L = 3600$ momentum points.

## 3. Calculation of Chern Numbers

The Chern number $C_n$ for the $n$-th band is given by the integral of the Berry curvature $\Omega_n(\boldsymbol{k})$ over the Brillouin zone (BZ):
$$
C_n = \frac{1}{2\pi} \int_{BZ} \Omega_n(\boldsymbol{k}) \, d^2 k
$$
On the discrete grid $\mathcal{K}$, we approximate this using the Fukui-Hatsugai-Suzuki method [3] (or equivalent lattice gauge theory approach).

**3.1 Link Variables**

For each band $n$, we define the link variables on the grid:
$$
U_\mu(\boldsymbol{k}) = \frac{\langle n(\boldsymbol{k}) | n(\boldsymbol{k} + \hat{\mu}) \rangle}{|\langle n(\boldsymbol{k}) | n(\boldsymbol{k} + \hat{\mu}) \rangle|}, \quad \mu \in \{1, 2\}
$$
where $| n(\boldsymbol{k}) \rangle$ is the eigenvector of the Hamiltonian $\mathcal{H}(\boldsymbol{k})$ for band $n$, and the neighbors $\boldsymbol{k}+\hat{1}$ and $\boldsymbol{k}+\hat{2}$ correspond to grid shifts along $\boldsymbol{b}_1$ and $\boldsymbol{b}_2$ respectively (with periodic boundary conditions).

**3.2 Field Strength (Plaquette Variable)**

The field strength $F_{12}(\boldsymbol{k})$ at each plaquette of the lattice is defined as the argument of the product of link variables around the unit square:
$$
F_{12}(\boldsymbol{k}) = \text{Im} \left[ \ln \left( U_1(\boldsymbol{k}) U_2(\boldsymbol{k} + \hat{1}) U_1(\boldsymbol{k} + \hat{2})^{-1} U_2(\boldsymbol{k})^{-1} \right) \right]
$$
where the logarithm is taken such that the result lies in $(-\pi, \pi]$.

**3.3 Chern Number Summation**

The Chern number is the sum of the field strengths over all plaquettes:
$$
C_n = \frac{1}{2\pi} \sum_{\boldsymbol{k} \in \mathcal{K}} F_{12}(\boldsymbol{k})
$$
This calculation is performed for the top three bands to obtain $C_{top}, C_{middle}, C_{bottom}$ (or $C_1, C_2, C_3$).

## 4. Calculation of the Quantum Metric Trace

For the top electron band (the band with the highest energy), we evaluate $\mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}\mathcal{G}$.

**4.1 Quantum Metric Definition**

The quantum metric $g_{ij}(\boldsymbol{k})$ for a band $n$ can be calculated from the eigenvectors $| n(\boldsymbol{k}) \rangle$. Using the partial derivatives approximation based on overlaps with neighboring cells:

We first define the quantity $Q_{ij}(\boldsymbol{k})$ which involves traces of the projector[4,5]. Since we are dealing with an isolated band (the top band), the projector is $P_{\boldsymbol{k}} = | n(\boldsymbol{k}) \rangle \langle n(\boldsymbol{k}) |$.

The trace of the metric is $\text{Tr}[g(\boldsymbol{k})] = g_{xx}(\boldsymbol{k}) + g_{yy}(\boldsymbol{k})$. It can be derived from the overlap matrices between neighboring unit cells.

Define the overlap matrix $W_{\mu}(\boldsymbol{k})$ involving the band projector:
$$
W_{\mu}(\boldsymbol{k}) = \langle \psi_{\boldsymbol{k}} | \psi_{\boldsymbol{k}+\hat{\mu}} \rangle \langle \psi_{\boldsymbol{k}+\hat{\mu}} | \psi_{\boldsymbol{k}} \rangle
$$
(Note: For a single band, this is $|U_\mu(\boldsymbol{k})|^2 = 1$, which is not sufficient. We must look at the broader connection.)

However, a robust numerical method for the integral of the quantum metric $\text{Tr}[g]$ utilizes the relationship between the spread functional and the position operator, or directly via the derivatives of the projector[6]:
$$
\text{Tr}[g(\boldsymbol{k})] \approx \frac{1}{\delta k^2} \sum_{\mu=1}^2 \left( 1 - \text{Tr} [P_{\boldsymbol{k}} P_{\boldsymbol{k}+\hat{\mu}}] \right)
$$
where $\delta k$ is the distance between adjacent grid points in the respective direction. Given our grid is defined by $\boldsymbol{b}_1, \boldsymbol{b}_2$ and size $L$, the spacing is $\boldsymbol{\delta}_1 = \boldsymbol{b}_1 / L$ and $\boldsymbol{\delta}_2 = \boldsymbol{b}_2 / L$.

More specifically, using the "band-flattening" or "eigenvector overlap" method suitable for discrete grids[7]:
We compute the "spread" contribution at each $\boldsymbol{k}$. Let the quantity to be integrated be $S(\boldsymbol{k})$.
For a single band $n$:
$$
\text{Tr}[g(\boldsymbol{k})] \approx \frac{1}{2} \sum_{\mu=1}^2 \frac{1}{|\boldsymbol{\delta}_\mu|^2} \left[ 1 - \left| \langle n(\boldsymbol{k}) | n(\boldsymbol{k} + \hat{\mu} \rangle \right|^2 \right]
$$
where $|\boldsymbol{\delta}_\mu|$ is the magnitude of the step vector. Since the grid is rectangular (not necessarily square) in reciprocal space coordinates determined by $\boldsymbol{b}_1, \boldsymbol{b}_2$, but the measure $d^2k$ depends on the area of the BZ cell.

The BZ area is $\mathcal{A}_{BZ} = |\boldsymbol{b}_1 \times \boldsymbol{b}_2|$.
The integration measure $d^2k$ corresponds to the area element associated with each grid point, which is $\mathcal{A}_{BZ} / L^2$.

Thus, the total target value $\mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}\mathcal{G}$ is:
$$
\mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}\mathcal{G} = \int d^2k \, \text{Tr}[g(\boldsymbol{k})] \approx \sum_{\boldsymbol{k} \in \mathcal{K}} \frac{\mathcal{A}_{BZ}}{L^2} \, \text{Tr}[g(\boldsymbol{k})]
$$

Substituting the approximation for $\text{Tr}[g(\boldsymbol{k})]$:
$$
\mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}\mathcal{G} \approx \sum_{\boldsymbol{k} \in \mathcal{K}} \frac{\mathcal{A}_{BZ}}{2 L^2} \left[ \frac{1}{|\boldsymbol{\delta}_1|^2} (1 - |\langle \psi_{\boldsymbol{k}} | \psi_{\boldsymbol{k}+\hat{1}} \rangle|^2) + \frac{1}{|\boldsymbol{\delta}_2|^2} (1 - |\langle \psi_{\boldsymbol{k}} | \psi_{\boldsymbol{k}+\hat{2}} \rangle|^2) \right]
$$

Note that $|\boldsymbol{\delta}_1| = |\boldsymbol{b}_1|/L$ and $|\boldsymbol{\delta}_2| = |\boldsymbol{b}_2|/L$. This simplifies the prefactor:
$$
\mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}\mathcal{G} \approx \frac{1}{2} \frac{\mathcal{A}_{BZ}}{L^2} \sum_{\boldsymbol{k} \in \mathcal{K}} \left[ \frac{L^2}{|\boldsymbol{b}_1|^2} (1 - O_1^2) + \frac{L^2}{|\boldsymbol{b}_2|^2} (1 - O_2^2) \right]
$$
$$
\mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}\mathcal{G} \approx \frac{1}{2} \sum_{\boldsymbol{k} \in \mathcal{K}} \left[ \frac{\mathcal{A}_{BZ}}{|\boldsymbol{b}_1|^2} (1 - O_1^2) + \frac{\mathcal{A}_{BZ}}{|\boldsymbol{b}_2|^2} (1 - O_2^2) \right]
$$
where $O_\mu = \langle n(\boldsymbol{k}) | n(\boldsymbol{k}+\hat{\mu}) \rangle$.

Since the Moiré lattice is a hexagonal Bravais lattice, we have symmetry constants. For a hexagonal lattice defined by $\boldsymbol{b}_1, \boldsymbol{b}_2$ (at 60 degrees):
$\mathcal{A}_{BZ} = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} |\boldsymbol{b}_1|^2$ (assuming $|\boldsymbol{b}_1| = |\boldsymbol{b}_2|$).
From our definitions:
$\boldsymbol{b}_1 = \boldsymbol{g}_1$, $|\boldsymbol{g}_1| = G$.
$\boldsymbol{b}_2 = \boldsymbol{g}_1 + \boldsymbol{g}_2$. Since $\boldsymbol{g}_1, \boldsymbol{g}_2$ are at 120 degrees, $|\boldsymbol{b}_2| = \sqrt{3} G$.
However, for the integration measure to be consistent with the provided grid definition, careful calculation of geometric factors is required. The provided grid covers the region spanned by $\boldsymbol{b}_1, \boldsymbol{b}_2$. The area is $|\boldsymbol{b}_1 \times \boldsymbol{b}_2|$.

The strategy to compute the value is:
1.  Diagonalize $\mathcal{H}(\boldsymbol{k})$ at all $\boldsymbol{k} \in \mathcal{K}$.
2.  Identify the indices corresponding to the top three bands (ordered by energy).
3.  Compute the Chern numbers for these three bands using the link variable summation described in section 3.3.
4.  For the top band, compute the overlaps $O_1, O_2$ at all $\boldsymbol{k}$.
5.  Sum these overlaps using the discrete approximation for the integral of the metric trace.
