Here is the mathematical description of the model for the quantum Fisher information (QFI) in the specified noisy distributed quantum sensing scenario.

# Model for Quantum Fisher Information in Noisy Distributed Quantum Sensing

## 1. System Definition and Parameters

We consider a quantum sensor network with the following structure:
*   **Network Geometry**: $d$ sensor nodes, identified by index $i \in \{1, \dots, d\}$.
*   **Local Resources**: Each node $i$ contains $n$ sensor qubits, identified by index $k \in \{1, \dots, n\}$. The total number of qubits is $N = nd$.
*   **Initial Probe State**: The network prepares an $N$-qubit noisy GHZ state distributed across all nodes. This state is described by a GHZ fidelity $F(n) = F k^{n-1}$.
*   **Local Parameters**: Each node $i$ has a local precession frequency $\omega^{(i)}$. The sensing evolution has duration $t$. The local phase accumulated is defined as:
    $$x_i = \omega^{(i)} t$$
*   **Estimation Target**: The goal is to estimate the scaled average of the local parameters:
    $$\theta_1 = \frac{1}{\sqrt{d}} \sum_{i=1}^d x_i$$
*   **Noise Parameters**:
    *   $\gamma$: Homogeneous single-qubit dephasing rate across the network.
    *   $q$: A dimensionless parameter encapsulating the dephasing noise over time $t$, defined as:
        $$q = \frac{1 + e^{-\gamma t}}{2}$$

## 2. Dynamics and State Evolution

The time evolution of the system's density matrix $\rho$ is governed by the Lindblad master equation provided in the setup. For the $k$-th qubit on the $i$-th node, the evolution is:

$$\frac{d}{dt}\rho = -i\frac{\omega^{(i)}}{2}\left[\sigma_z^{(i,k)},\rho\right] + \frac{\gamma}{2}\left(\sigma_z^{(i,k)}\rho \sigma_z^{(i,k)} - \rho\right)$$

Since the Lindblad operators for different qubits commute, the noisy channel for the entire system can be treated as the tensor product of single-qubit channels. The unitary part of the evolution encodes the parameters, and the dissipative part causes dephasing.

### 2.1. Effective Dephasing Probability

The dephasing term in the Lindblad equation corresponds to a phase-damping channel. For a single qubit, this channel can be represented in the Pauli basis as effectively applying a $\sigma_z$ flip with probability $p$, while leaving the state unchanged with probability $1-p$.

By comparing the solution of the Lindblad equation for a single qubit to the Kraus operator representation of a phase-flip channel, we derive the relationship between the dephasing rate $\gamma$, time $t$, the probability $p$, and the given variable $q$.

The off-diagonal term of a density matrix $\rho_{01}$ decays as $e^{-\gamma t}$.
For a phase-flip channel with probability $p$, $\rho_{01} \to (1-2p)\rho_{01}$.
Equating these:
$$1 - 2p = e^{-\gamma t}$$
$$2p = 1 - e^{-\gamma t}$$
$$p = \frac{1 - e^{-\gamma t}}{2}$$

Observing the definition of $q$:
$$q = \frac{1 + e^{-\gamma t}}{2}$$
We find that the probability of a phase flip (dephasing error) is:
$$p = 1 - q$$
and the probability of no phase flip (coherence preservation factor modulo sign) is:
$$1 - 2p = 2q - 1$$

### 2.2. Effect on the GHZ State

The initial probe state is a superposition of the form $\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(|\mathbf{0}\rangle + |\mathbf{1}\rangle)$ plus mixed components due to imperfect fidelity $F(n)$.
Under the dephasing noise acting independently on all $N = nd$ qubits, the coherence between the all-zeros and all-ones components is scaled by the factor $(1-2p)^N$.

Introducing the variable $\lambda$ to represent the scaling of the off-diagonal terms (effective coherence) due to the *dynamics* noise:
$$\lambda_{dynamic} = (2q - 1)^{nd}$$

## 3. Quantum Fisher Information (QFI) Derivation

We calculate the QFI for the parameter $\theta_1$.

### 3.1. Generator of $\theta_1$

The unitary evolution corresponding to the parameters is $U = \bigotimes_{i,k} \exp(-i \frac{\omega^{(i)} t}{2} \sigma_z^{(i,k)})$.
The generator of the parameter $x_i$ is $H_i = \frac{1}{2} \sum_{k=1}^n \sigma_z^{(i,k)}$.
We want to estimate $\theta_1 = \frac{1}{\sqrt{d}} \sum_i x_i$. The generator for $\theta_1$, denoted as $G_{\theta_1}$, is the linear combination of the generators for $x_i$:
$$G_{\theta_1} = \sum_{i=1}^d \frac{\partial \theta_1}{\partial x_i} H_i = \sum_{i=1}^d \frac{1}{\sqrt{d}} \left( \frac{1}{2} \sum_{k=1}^n \sigma_z^{(i,k)} \right) = \frac{1}{2\sqrt{d}} \underbrace{\sum_{i=1}^d \sum_{k=1}^n \sigma_z^{(i,k)}}_{\hat{S}_z^{total}}$$
where $\hat{S}_z^{total}$ is the collective spin operator in the $z$-direction for the entire network of $nd$ qubits.

### 3.2. QFI for Noisy GHZ-like States

The Quantum Fisher Information for a mixed state $\rho$ with respect to a parameter $\theta$ generated by $G$ is given by:
$$F_Q = 2 \sum_{i,j} \frac{|\langle i | G | j \rangle|^2 (\mu_i - \mu_j)^2}{\mu_i + \mu_j}$$
where $\mu_i$ are the eigenvalues of the state $\rho$ and $|i\rangle$ are the eigenvectors.

For a state that is effectively a GHZ state mixed with white noise (or a depolarized GHZ), the state density matrix is spanned by the subspace $\{ |\mathbf{0}\rangle, |\mathbf{1}\rangle \}$ and the orthogonal subspace. The eigenvectors relevant for the estimation are typically $|\mathbf{0}\rangle$ and $|\mathbf{1}\rangle$.

*   Let the initial state have a specific form based on the fidelity $F(n)$. Without loss of generality for the scaling dependence, we consider the state to be composed of a "GHZ-like" component $\rho_{GHZ}$ and an orthogonal component.
*   The relevant matrix element for the QFI is determined by the action of $G_{\theta_1}$ between $|\mathbf{0}\rangle$ and $|\mathbf{1}\rangle$.
*   Note that $G_{\theta_1} |\mathbf{0}\rangle = \frac{nd}{2\sqrt{d}} |\mathbf{0}\rangle$ and $G_{\theta_1} |\mathbf{1}\rangle = -\frac{nd}{2\sqrt{d}} |\mathbf{1}\rangle$.
*   The off-diagonal matrix element squared is:
    $$|\langle \mathbf{1} | G_{\theta_1} | \mathbf{0} \rangle|^2 = \left( \frac{nd}{2\sqrt{d}} \right)^2 = \frac{n^2 d}{4}$$

The total QFI scales with the square of this matrix element and the "visibility" or "coherence" of the state.
1.  **Initial Coherence**: The fidelity $F(n) = F k^{n-1}$ characterizes the initial weight of the GHZ component vs the maximally mixed state. In a depolarized GHZ model with fidelity $F$, the off-diagonal term is roughly $F(n)$ (ignoring scaling constants absorbed into $F$).
2.  **Decay due to Dynamics**: The dynamics noise multiplies the off-diagonal term by $(2q - 1)^{nd}$.

Assuming the state is such that the QFI is proportional to the square of the total coherence (which is the standard result for GHZ states under dephasing), we have:
$$F_Q \propto \left[ \text{Initial Coherence} \times \text{Dynamics Coherence} \right] \times | \text{Matrix Element} |^2$$

### 3.3. Final Expression

Combining the factors:
*   The "visibility" is proportional to the fidelity $F(n) = F k^{n-1}$.
*   The attenuated visibility from sensing is $F(n) \cdot (2q - 1)^{nd}$.
*   The squared matrix element factor provides the scaling $n^2 d$ (normalized appropriately for $\theta_1$).

The QFI for the noisy GHZ state estimating the average phase is:
$$F_Q = n^2 d \left( F k^{n-1} (2q - 1)^{nd} \right)$$

However, in the standard derivation of QFI for mixed GHZ states (where the density matrix is $\frac{1-p}{2}(|0^n\rangle\langle 1^n| + h.c.) + \dots$), the QFI is $n^2 (1-2p)^2$. Here, we have two sources of mixedness: initial preparation fidelity $F(n)$ (playing the role of initial state weight) and dynamic dephasing $(2q-1)^{nd}$.

The "effective" coherence factor is $F(n) (2q - 1)^{nd}$.
Since QFI is proportional to the *square* of the coherence for single-axis estimation:
$$F_Q \approx n^2 d \left[ F(n) \right]^2 \left[ (2q - 1)^{nd} \right]^2$$

Wait, let's refine the scaling of the dynamic term.
The Lindblad dynamics acts on the density matrix elements directly. The off-diagonal term $\rho_{0,1}$ decays as $e^{-\text{coefficient} \cdot t}$.
For $N$ qubits, the accumulated phase is accumulated $N$ times faster in the off-diagonal term.
The magnitude of the off-diagonal element $\text{Im}(\rho)$ becomes $\text{Im}(\rho_{initial}) \times (2q - 1)^{nd}$.
The QFI is inversely proportional to the variance of the generator weighted by the state's purity/geometry. For GHZ, $F_Q = n^2 \times (\text{coherence factor})^2$.
The coherence factor after dynamics is $F(n) \times (2q - 1)^{nd}$.
Thus:
$$F_Q = d \cdot n^2 \cdot \left[ F(n) (2q - 1)^{nd} \right]^2$$
(Note: The factor $d$ in front comes from the definition of the average $\theta_1$ scaling $\omega$ by $1/\sqrt{d}$? Let's check).

Let's re-verify the generator scaling.
We estimate $\theta_1 = \frac{1}{\sqrt{d}} \sum x_i$.
The QFI for $\vec{\theta}$ in a GHZ state is $Q_{\mu\nu} = 4 \text{Var}(H_\mu) \delta_{\mu\nu} \times (\text{purity term})$.
Actually, for the average parameter $\theta_1$, $H_{\theta_1} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{d}} \sum_k \frac{S_z^{k}}{2}$.
Variance of $S_z^{total}$ for GHZ is $(nd)^2/4$.
So $\text{Var}(H_{\theta_1}) = \frac{1}{d} \frac{(nd)^2}{4} = \frac{n^2 d}{4}$.
The QFI for a pure state is $4 \text{Var}(H) = n^2 d$.
Now we introduce noise. The noise scales the state off-diagonals.
The total coherence factor is $F(n) \times (2q - 1)^{nd}$.
QFI scales with the square of the coherence.
Final expression:
$$F_Q = n^2 d \left( F(n) \right)^2 (2q - 1)^{2nd}$$

Substituting $F(n) = F k^{n-1}$:
$$F_Q = n^2 d F^2 k^{2(n-1)} (2q - 1)^{2nd}$$

Mathematical Description for the Model

## 1. System Definition

We consider a distributed quantum sensor network consisting of $d$ nodes. Each node $i$ contains $n$ qubits, making the total number of qubits $N_{tot} = nd$. The objective is to estimate the scaled average parameter $\theta_1$, defined as:
$$ \theta_1 = \frac{1}{\sqrt{d}} \sum_{i=1}^d x_i $$
where $x_i = \omega^{(i)} t$ is the accumulated phase at node $i$.

The system utilizes a noisy Greenberger-Horne-Zeilinger (GHZ) state as an initial probe, characterized by a fidelity:
$$ F(n) = F k^{n-1} $$
The dynamics are governed by local dephasing channels described by the Lindblad equation. We define the variable $q$ to characterize the noise strength over time $t$:
$$ q = \frac{1 + e^{-\gamma t}}{2} $$

## 2. Model Derivation Steps

### Step 1: Characterization of the Noise Channel
The Lindblad equation for a single qubit is:
$$ \frac{d}{dt}\rho = -i\frac{\omega}{2}[\sigma_z, \rho] + \frac{\gamma}{2}(\sigma_z \rho \sigma_z - \rho) $$
This describes dephasing noise. The off-diagonal elements of the density matrix $\rho_{01}$ decay exponentially as $\rho_{01}(t) = \rho_{01}(0) e^{-\gamma t}$. This can be mapped to a phase-flip channel with a probability of error $p$.
For a phase-flip channel, $\rho_{01} \to (1-2p)\rho_{01}$.
Comparing this to the Lindblad solution:
$$ 1 - 2p = e^{-\gamma t} \implies p = \frac{1 - e^{-\gamma t}}{2} $$
Using the provided variable $q = \frac{1 + e^{-\gamma t}}{2}$, the probability of no error (the factor preserving coherence, aside from the deterministic phase evolution) is:
$$ 1 - 2p = 2q - 1 $$

### Step 2: Evolution of the $N$-Qubit State
Since the dephasing acts independently on all $nd$ qubits, the total dephasing factor for the GHZ coherence term (which involves all qubits) is the product of the individual factors.
Let $\alpha$ be the initial magnitude of the coherence of the GHZ state (which is related to the fidelity $F(n)$).
The coherence term $|\Psi_0\rangle\langle\Psi_1|$ decays by a factor of $(1-2p)$ for each qubit.
The total factor for $nd$ qubits is $(1-2p)^{nd}$.
Substituting the expression in terms of $q$:
$$ \text{Decay Factor} = (2q - 1)^{nd} $$
The final coherence magnitude of the state is thus $\alpha_{final} = \alpha_{initial} \times (2q - 1)^{nd}$.
Given the initial noisy GHZ state has fidelity $F(n)$, the initial coherence is proportional to $F(n)$.
$$ \alpha_{final} \propto F k^{n-1} (2q - 1)^{nd} $$

### Step 3: Quantum Fisher Information (QFI) Calculation
The QFI for a parameter $\theta$ generated by an observable $G$ in a state $\rho$ is related to the variance and purity. For a GHZ state (or a classically mixed state including a GHZ component) estimating a global phase, the QFI scales with the square of the coherence (off-diagonal term magnitude) and the square of the total spin involved.

**The Generator:**
We are estimating $\theta_1 = \frac{1}{\sqrt{d}} \sum x_i$.
The generator for $x_i$ is $H_i = \frac{1}{2} \sum_{k=1}^n \sigma_z^{(i,k)}$.
The generator for $\theta_1$ is:
$$ G_{\theta_1} = \sum_{i=1}^d \frac{1}{\sqrt{d}} H_i = \frac{1}{2\sqrt{d}} \sum_{i=1}^d \sum_{k=1}^n \sigma_z^{(i,k)} $$
The variance of this generator for an $nd$-qubit GHZ state is:
$$ \text{Var}(G_{\theta_1}) = \frac{1}{4d} \text{Var}\left(\sum \sigma_z\right) = \frac{1}{4d} (nd)^2 = \frac{n^2 d}{4} $$
The QFI for a *pure* GHZ state would be $4 \text{Var}(G_{\theta_1}) = n^2 d$.

**Effect of Noise on QFI:**
For mixed states of this form (dephased GHZ), the QFI is proportional to the square of the coherence factor.
$$ F_Q \propto (\text{Coherence})^2 \times (\text{Pure State QFI}) $$
$$ F_Q = \left[ F(n) (2q - 1)^{nd} \right]^2 \times n^2 d $$

### Step 4: Final Expression
Substituting $F(n) = F k^{n-1}$, we obtain the final mathematical description of the quantum Fisher information for $\theta_1$.

$$ F_Q = n^2 d F^2 k^{2(n-1)} (2q - 1)^{2nd} $$
