# Mathematical Model for B-L Vector Dark Matter Search with LIGO

## 1. Introduction and Physical Setup

This model describes the search for a vector field dark matter candidate, specifically a $B-L$ (Baryon number minus Lepton number) gauge boson, using the LIGO interferometer. The detection mechanism relies on the coupling of this vector field ($A^\mu$) to the Standard Model $B-L$ current. This interaction exerts a force on the interferometer's test masses (mirrors). By doping one of the mirrors (the outer mirror), we create a differential charge-to-mass ratio between the inner and outer mirrors. This generates a differential force that acts as a signal strain superimposed on the instrument's noise.

The goal is to determine the smallest probeable coupling constant, $\epsilon_{B-L}$, given LIGO's strain sensitivity, a 13-year observation time ($T_{\text{obs}}$), a signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) of 1, and specific doping scenarios parameterized by $\delta q$.

## 2. Model Parameters

The model operates based on the following constants and parameters:

*   **Fundamental Constants:**
    *   Electron charge: $e \approx 1.602 \times 10^{-19} \text{ C}$
    *   Neutron mass: $m_n \approx 1.675 \times 10^{-27} \text{ kg}$
    *   Speed of light: $c \approx 3.0 \times 10^8 \text{ m/s}$
    *   Local dark matter density: $\rho_{\text{DM}} \approx 0.4 \text{ GeV/cm}^3 \approx 6.4 \times 10^{-22} \text{ kg/m}^3$
*   **Instrumental Parameters (LIGO):**
    *   Frequency of interest: $f = 250 \text{ Hz}$
    *   Strain sensitivity (amplitude spectral density): $h_n(f) = 3 \times 10^{-24} \text{ Hz}^{-1/2}$
    *   Observation time: $T_{\text{obs}} = 13 \text{ years} \approx 4.1 \times 10^8 \text{ s}$
    *   Arm length (effective for phase accumulation or light storage time): $L \approx 4 \text{ km}$
*   **Dark Matter and Mirror Parameters:**
    *   Dark matter field velocity dispersion (virial velocity): $v_0 \approx 10^{-3}c \approx 300 \text{ km/s}$
    *   Base charge-to-mass ratio (inner mirror): $(Q_D/M)_{\text{inner}} \approx 0.5/m_n$
    *   Additional charge-to-mass ratio from doping (outer mirror): $\delta(Q_D/M)_{\text{outer}} \approx \delta q/m_n$
    *   Scenarios for $\delta q$: $\{0.074, 6 \times 10^{-3}, 5 \times 10^{-4}\}$

## 3. Theoretical Derivation Steps

### Step 1: The Dark Matter Field Amplitude

The vector field $A^\mu$ constitutes all the dark matter. Its energy density oscillates at a frequency $\omega \approx m_{A}c^2/\hbar$. For a vector field of mass $m_A$, the amplitude of the field $|\vec{A}|$ is related to the local dark matter energy density $\rho_{\text{DM}}$. In the non-relativistic limit, the energy density is given by:

$$
\rho_{\text{DM}} \approx \frac{1}{2} m_A^2 \omega^2 |\vec{A}|^2 \approx \frac{1}{2} m_A^2 c^2 |\vec{A}|^2
$$

Solving for the field amplitude $|\vec{A}|$:

$$
|\vec{A}| \approx \frac{\sqrt{2\rho_{\text{DM}}}}{m_A c}
$$

### Step 2: Force on the Mirrors

The interaction Lagrangian is given by $\mathcal{L} \supset -\epsilon_{B-L}e J^\mu_{B-L} A_\mu$. This leads to a "dark" electric field $\vec{E}_D = -\partial_t \vec{A} \approx \omega |\vec{A}| \cos(\omega t) \approx m_A c |\vec{A}|$. The force $\vec{F}$ exerted on a mirror with total dark charge $Q_D$ and mass $M$ is:

$$
\vec{F} = Q_D \vec{E}_D \epsilon_{B-L}
$$

The corresponding acceleration $\vec{a} = \vec{F}/M$ is:

$$
\vec{a} = \frac{Q_D}{M} \epsilon_{B-L} \vec{E}_D
$$

Substituting $\vec{E}_D$ and the expression for $|\vec{A}|$ from Step 1:

$$
\vec{a} \approx \frac{Q_D}{M} \epsilon_{B-L} (m_A c) \left(\frac{\sqrt{2\rho_{\text{DM}}}}{m_A c}\right) \cos(\omega t) = \frac{Q_D}{M} \epsilon_{B-L} \sqrt{2\rho_{\text{DM}}} \cos(\omega t)
$$

Note that the dependence on the particle mass $m_A$ cancels out in the acceleration expression. The observed signal is driven by the coupling $\epsilon_{B-L}$, the charge-to-mass ratio, and the local dark matter density.

### Step 3: Differential Signal Strain

The effective differential strain $h(t)$ observed by the interferometer is generated by the relative acceleration between the two mirrors. The inner mirror has charge-to-mass ratio $Q_{\text{in}}/M = 0.5/m_n$. The outer mirror is doped, such that its charge-to-mass ratio is $Q_{\text{out}}/M = 0.5/m_n + \delta q/m_n$.

The differential acceleration $a_{\text{diff}}$ is the difference between the accelerations of the two mirrors (assuming the field is coherent over the scale of the interferometer):

$$
a_{\text{diff}} \approx \epsilon_{B-L} \sqrt{2\rho_{\text{DM}}} \left| \frac{Q_{\text{out}}}{M} - \frac{Q_{\text{in}}}{M} \right| = \epsilon_{B-L} \sqrt{2\rho_{\text{DM}}} \left| \frac{\delta q}{m_n} \right|
$$

The strain $h(t)$ is related to the acceleration by the square of the angular frequency ($h \sim a/L \omega^2$) for a static response, or more commonly for resonant signals, via the modulation at frequency $\omega$. However, for a force driving at a frequency $\omega$, the displacement $x(t)$ is $a(\omega)/\omega^2$. Since the strain $h = \Delta L / L = x/L$, we have:

$$
h(\omega) \approx \frac{a_{\text{diff}}(\omega)}{L \omega^2}
$$

Substituting the expression for $a_{\text{diff}}$ and using $\omega = 2\pi f$:

$$
h(f) \approx \frac{\epsilon_{B-L} \sqrt{2\rho_{\text{DM}}}}{L (2\pi f)^2} \frac{\delta q}{m_n}
$$

This represents the amplitude of the signal strain at frequency $f$.

### Step 4: Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNR)

LIGO is a shot-noise limited instrument at high frequencies (around 250 Hz). The sensitivity is characterized by the amplitude spectral density of the strain noise, $h_n(f)$.

For a signal integrated over an observation time $T_{\text{obs}}$, the effective noise level improves roughly as $1/\sqrt{T_{\text{obs}}}$ compared to the noise power spectral density $S_h(f) = h_n(f)^2$. The signal power spectral density $S_h(f)$ relates to the amplitude by $\sqrt{S_h(f) \Delta f} \approx h(f)$ where $\Delta f \approx 1/T_{\text{obs}}$ is the frequency resolution.

However, it is often simpler to compare the signal amplitude to the effective noise amplitude scaled by the number of averages. The characteristic strain of the noise over time $T$ is effectively reduced. A robust estimate for SNR of a narrowband signal in white noise is:

$$
\text{SNR} \approx \frac{h(f)}{h_n(f)} \sqrt{T_{\text{obs}} f}
$$

Alternatively, equating the power:
$$
\text{SNR}^2 = \frac{h(f)^2 T_{\text{obs}}}{S_h(f)} \times \text{N}_{\text{averages}} \dots \implies \text{SNR} \approx \frac{|h(f)|}{h_n(f)} \sqrt{T_{\text{obs}} \cdot \text{bandwidth}}
$$

For a coherent integration over $T_{\text{obs}}$, the bandwidth is $\Delta f = 1/T_{\text{obs}}$.
$$
\text{SNR} \approx \frac{|h(f)|}{h_n(f)} \sqrt{T_{\text{obs}} \Delta f} = \frac{|h(f)|}{h_n(f)} \sqrt{T_{\text{obs}} \frac{1}{T_{\text{obs}}}} \dots \text{This suggests unit sensitivity at arbitrarily long times for fixed } h(f).
$$

A more appropriate scaling for a matched filter or frequency-domain analysis of a continuous wave is:
$$
\text{SNR} = \frac{h(f)}{h_n(f)} \sqrt{T_{\text{obs}}}
$$
(In some derivations using power spectral density, the factor is simply $\sqrt{T_{\text{obs}}}$ because the noise variance decreases by $1/T$).
Let's use the standard relation for amplitude spectral density integration:
The noise in a bin of width $\Delta f = 1/T$ is $h_{\text{bin}} = h_n(f) \sqrt{\Delta f} = h_n(f) / \sqrt{T}$.
The signal is a coherent line with amplitude $h(f)$.
Thus:
$$
\text{SNR} = \frac{h(f)}{h_n(f) / \sqrt{T_{\text{obs}}}} = \frac{h(f) \sqrt{T_{\text{obs}}}}{h_n(f)}
$$

We set $\text{SNR} = 1$ to find the smallest probeable $\epsilon_{B-L}$, denoted as $\epsilon_{\text{min}}$.

## 4. Final Mathematical Model

Combining the equations from Steps 3 and 4, we set SNR = 1 and solve for $\epsilon_{B-L}$:

$$
1 = \frac{\epsilon_{\text{min}} \sqrt{2\rho_{\text{DM}}}}{L (2\pi f)^2} \frac{\delta q}{m_n} \frac{\sqrt{T_{\text{obs}}}}{h_n(f)}
$$

Rearranging for $\epsilon_{\text{min}}$:

$$
\epsilon_{\text{min}} = \frac{L (2\pi f)^2 m_n h_n(f)}{\sqrt{2\rho_{\text{DM}}} \delta q \sqrt{T_{\text{obs}}}}
$$

This is the core mathematical description of the model. It calculates the limit on the $B-L$ coupling as a function of the differential doping parameter $\delta q$.

**Summary of the model steps:**
1.  **Input:** $\delta q$, fundamental constants ($\rho_{\text{DM}}, m_n, e$, etc.), and LIGO parameters ($L, f, h_n, T_{\text{obs}}$).
2.  **Calculate:** The numerator containing the interferometer's response scaling (mass times acceleration frequency squared times noise).
3.  **Calculate:** The denominator containing the dark matter drive and the integration gain (density times doping factor times square root of time).
4.  **Output:** $\epsilon_{\min}$.

## 5. Application to Scenarios

We apply the model to the three requested scenarios for $\delta q$.

**Constants Summary:**
*   $L = 4000 \text{ m}$
*   $f = 250 \text{ Hz} \implies (2\pi f)^2 \approx 2.47 \times 10^6 \text{ s}^{-2}$
*   $m_n \approx 1.675 \times 10^{-27} \text{ kg}$
*   $h_n(f) = 3 \times 10^{-24} \text{ Hz}^{-1/2}$
*   $\rho_{\text{DM}} \approx 6.4 \times 10^{-22} \text{ kg/m}^3 \implies \sqrt{2\rho_{\text{DM}}} \approx \sqrt{12.8 \times 10^{-22}} \approx 3.58 \times 10^{-11} \text{ kg}^{1/2} \text{ m}^{-3/2}$
*   $T_{\text{obs}} = 13 \text{ years} \approx 4.1 \times 10^8 \text{ s} \implies \sqrt{T_{\text{obs}}} \approx 2.02 \times 10^4 \text{ s}^{1/2}$

**Numerator Constant ($C_{\text{num}}$):**
$$
C_{\text{num}} = L \cdot (2\pi f)^2 \cdot m_n \cdot h_n(f)
$$
$$
C_{\text{num}} = (4000) \cdot (2.47 \times 10^6) \cdot (1.675 \times 10^{-27}) \cdot (3 \times 10^{-24})
$$
$$
C_{\text{num}} \approx 4000 \cdot 2.47 \cdot 1.675 \cdot 3 \cdot 10^{6 - 27 - 24} \approx 49674 \times 10^{-45} \approx 4.97 \times 10^{-41} \text{ kg m Hz}^{-1/2}
$$

**Denominator Factor ($C_{\text{den, common}}$):**
$$
C_{\text{den}} = \sqrt{2\rho_{\text{DM}}} \cdot \sqrt{T_{\text{obs}}} \approx (3.58 \times 10^{-11}) \cdot (2.02 \times 10^4) \approx 7.23 \times 10^{-7} \text{ kg}^{1/2} \text{ m}^{-3/2} \text{ s}^{1/2}
$$

**Combined Constant ($K = C_{\text{num}} / C_{\text{den}}$):**
$$
K = \frac{4.97 \times 10^{-41}}{7.23 \times 10^{-7}} \approx 6.87 \times 10^{-35}
$$

The limits are calculated as $\epsilon_{\min} = K / \delta q$.

### Scenario 1: $\delta q = 0.074$

$$
\epsilon_{\text{min}} = \frac{6.87 \times 10^{-35}}{0.074} \approx 9.28 \times 10^{-34}
$$

### Scenario 2: $\delta q = 6 \times 10^{-3}$

$$
\epsilon_{\text{min}} = \frac{6.87 \times 10^{-35}}{6 \times 10^{-3}} \approx 1.15 \times 10^{-32}
$$

### Scenario 3: $\delta q = 5 \times 10^{-4}$

$$
\epsilon_{\text{min}} = \frac{6.87 \times 10^{-35}}{5 \times 10^{-4}} \approx 1.37 \times 10^{-31}
$$

## 6. Results

Based on the described physical model, the smallest probeable $B-L$ coupling constants ($\epsilon_{B-L}$) for an SNR of 1 over 13 years at 250 Hz are:

*   For $\delta q = 0.074$:
    $$ \epsilon_{B-L} \approx 9.3 \times 10^{-34} $$

*   For $\delta q = 6 \times 10^{-3}$:
    $$ \epsilon_{B-L} \approx 1.2 \times 10^{-32} $$

*   For $\delta q = 5 \times 10^{-4}$:
    $$ \epsilon_{B-L} \approx 1.4 \times 10^{-31} $$

These values assume coherent integration of the signal over the 13-year period and that the noise is dominated by the white strain noise floor at 250 Hz.
