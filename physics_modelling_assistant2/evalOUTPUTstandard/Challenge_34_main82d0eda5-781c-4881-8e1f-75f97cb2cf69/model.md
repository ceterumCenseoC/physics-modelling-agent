# Model Description: Thermodynamic Limit of the Two-Point Correlation Function in a Star-Shaped qMPS

## 1. Physical Model and Circuit Structure

The system under consideration is a quantum Matrix Product State (qMPS) generated by a specific unitary circuit applied to a system of $N+1$ qubits.

**The Setup:**
- We define a set of qubits labelled $j = 0, 1, \dots, N$.
- The initial state is the product state of all qubits in the $|0\rangle$ state:
  $$
  |\psi_{\text{init}}\rangle = |0\rangle_0 \otimes |0\rangle_1 \otimes \cdots \otimes |0\rangle_N.
  $$
- The system evolves via a sequence of two-qunitary gates $U_{jk}$ applied in a specific order.

**The Gate Definition:**
The fundamental interaction is defined by the two-qubit gate $U_{jk}$ acting on qubits $(j, k)$:
$$
U_{jk} = e^{-i b (X_j X_k + Z_j Z_k)/2} e^{-i a X_k/2}.
$$
Here, $X$ and $Z$ are the standard Pauli matrices:
$$
X = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}, \quad Z = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 \end{pmatrix}.
$$
The parameters $a$ and $b$ are rotation angles constrained to the range $0 < a, b < \pi/2$.

**The Circuit Topology:**
The gates are applied sequentially in a "star" topology, where qubit $0$ acts as a central ancilla interacting with every other qubit in order. The evolution sequence is:
1.  Apply $U_{01}$ to qubits $(0, 1)$.
2.  Apply $U_{02}$ to qubits $(0, 2)$.
...
k+1. Apply $U_{0k}$ to qubits $(0, k)$.
...
N+1. Apply $U_{0N}$ to qubits $(0, N)$.

The final state of the system, $|\psi_N\rangle$, is:
$$
|\psi_N\rangle = U_{0N} \cdots U_{03} U_{02} U_{01} |\psi_{\text{init}}\rangle.
$$
We are interested in the properties of this state in the *thermodynamic limit*, defined as $N \rightarrow \infty$. Specifically, we seek the two-point correlation function between physical qubits $N-2$ and $N$.

## 2. Analytic Decomposition of the Unitary Gate

To evaluate the correlation function, we first analyze the structure of the gate $U_{jk}$. Using the identity $e^{-i \theta O} = \cos\theta \mathbb{I} - i \sin\theta O$ for $O^2 = \mathbb{I}$, we can decompose the exponential terms.

**First term:** $e^{-i b (X_j X_k + Z_j Z_k)/2}$.
Note that $(X_j X_k + Z_j Z_k)^2 = 2 \mathbb{I}_{jk}$. Therefore, we can write this term as:
$$
e^{-i b (X_j X_k + Z_j Z_k)/2} = \cos\left(\frac{b}{\sqrt{2}}\right) \mathbb{I}_{jk} - i \sin\left(\frac{b}{\sqrt{2}}\right) \frac{X_j X_k + Z_j Z_k}{\sqrt{2}}.
$$

**Second term:** $e^{-i a X_k/2}$.
This is a standard rotation operator around the X-axis for the target qubit $k$:
$$
e^{-i a X_k/2} = \cos\left(\frac{a}{2}\right) \mathbb{I}_k - i \sin\left(\frac{a}{2}\right) X_k.
$$

**Matrix Representation in Computational Basis:**
Expressing the gate in the joint basis $\{|00\rangle, |01\rangle, |10\rangle, |11\rangle\}$ (where the first digit corresponds to qubit $j$ and the second to $k$), we obtain the matrix elements $U_{\sigma_j \sigma_k, \sigma'_j \sigma'_k}$.

The matrix representation of $U_{0k}$ (where $j=0$ and $k$ is the target) is explicitly given by evaluating the matrix product of the two operators derived above. While we do not compute the numerical values here, the model requires the explicit matrix elements $\mathcal{U}_{\alpha\beta}^{\gamma\delta}(a, b)$ where $\alpha, \gamma \in \{0,1\}$ denote states of the ancilla (qubit 0) and $\beta, \delta \in \{0,1\}$ denote states of the target qubit $k$.

The state of the system can be viewed as a Matrix Product State (MPS) where the central qubit's state defines the "bond" index connecting the sites $1, \dots, N$. Because the bond dimension is $\chi=2$, the state of the system can be fully characterized by the evolution of the central qubit.

## 3. Transfer Matrix Formulation in the Thermodynamic Limit

The expectation value of a local observable $\mathcal{O}$ in a Matrix Product State can be computed using the transfer matrix formalism (as described in Ueda et al., *Uniform Matrix Product State in the Thermodynamic Limit*).

For a system of $N+1$ qubits generated by this star-shaped circuit, the "unit cell" corresponds to the application of the gate $U_{0k}$ to a new pair of qubits. Because the gate sequence defines the state, we can construct a $4 \times 4$ transfer matrix $\mathcal{T}$ that describes the propagation of information along the chain of physical qubits $1, \dots, N$.

Let the state of the ancilla qubit after interactions up to qubit $k-1$ be described by a density matrix $\rho_{k-1}$. The action of applying $U_{0k}$ to the ancilla (in state $\rho_{k-1}$) and a fresh physical qubit $k$ (in state $|0\rangle\langle0|$) results in a new ancilla state $\rho_k$ after tracing out the physical qubit $k$.

However, to compute correlations, we must retain the relevant quantum information. We construct the transfer matrix $\mathcal{T}$ such that its elements are defined by the partial trace of the gate evolution:
$$
\mathcal{T} = \text{Tr}_{\text{phys}} \left[ (\mathbb{I} \otimes \langle 0 |) U_{0k} (|0\rangle \otimes \rho_{k-1}) U_{0k}^\dagger (\mathbb{I} \otimes |0\rangle) \right].
$$
In a basis-independent form, we can view $\mathcal{T}$ as a super-operator mapping operators on the ancilla space to new operators on the ancilla space. Since the ancilla is a qubit, $\mathcal{T}$ acts on the vector space of $2 \times 2$ matrices, effectively making $\mathcal{T}$ a $4 \times 4$ matrix.

Explicitly, using the gate coefficients from the decomposition in Section 2, we can compute the components of $\mathcal{T}$. Let $\sigma^\mu$ with $\mu = 0, x, y, z$ denote the Pauli basis (with $\sigma^0 = \mathbb{I}$). The transfer matrix elements $\mathcal{T}^{\mu}_{\nu}$ are determined by:
$$
\mathcal{T}[\sigma^\nu] = \sum_\mu \mathcal{T}^{\mu}_{\nu} \sigma^\mu.
$$

Because of the rotational symmetry of the interactions, one can show that $\mathcal{T}$ is block-diagonal.
The transfer matrix allows us to calculate expectation values. If $M$ is an operator associated with a site (projected onto the relevant expectation value), then the expectation value for a single site would be:
$$
\langle \mathcal{O} \rangle = \lim_{N \to \infty} \frac{\text{Tr}[M \mathcal{T}^N \rho_0]}{\text{Tr}[\mathcal{T}^N \rho_0]}.
$$
In the thermodynamic limit, only the dominant eigenvector of $\mathcal{T}$ (corresponding to the eigenvalue 1) contributes to the ratio.

## 4. Computation of the Two-Point Correlation Function

We want to compute the correlation function $C(r) = \langle Z_{k} Z_{k+r} \rangle$. In the specific problem, $r=2$ and we look at the horizon limit.

To compute this, we must modify the transfer matrix to account for the insertion of the $Z$ operator at the relevant physical sites.

1.  **Single-site operator insertion:**
    Define the matrix $W_Z$ which corresponds to the partial trace involving the $Z$ operator acting on the physical qubit $k$:
    $$
    W_Z = \text{Tr}_{\text{phys}} \left[ Z_k \cdot (\mathbb{I} \otimes \langle 0 |) U_{0k} (\cdot) U_{0k}^\dagger (\mathbb{I} \otimes |0\rangle) \right].
    $$
    This matrix $W_Z$ acts on the ancilla space similarly to $\mathcal{T}$, but incorporates the physics of the $Z$ measurement.

2.  **Disconnected part:**
    The correlation function $\langle Z_{N-2} Z_N \rangle$ contains the product of the individual expectation values: $\langle Z_{N-2} \rangle \langle Z_N \rangle$. In the transfer matrix language with dominant eigenvector $|\Lambda\rangle$, this is:
    $$
    \langle Z \rangle^2 = \left( \frac{\langle L | W_Z | R \rangle}{\langle L | R \rangle} \right)^2,
    $$
    where $|R\rangle$ and $\langle L|$ are the right and left dominant eigenvectors of $\mathcal{T}$ respectively (normalized such that $\langle L | R \rangle = 1$).

3.  **Connected correlator:**
    To include the interaction information flowing between sites $N-2$ and $N$, we look at the separation $r=2$. The correlation function in the thermodynamic limit is given by:
    $$
    \lim_{N \to \infty} \langle Z_{N-2} Z_N \rangle = \frac{\langle L | W_Z \mathcal{T} W_Z | R \rangle}{\langle L | R \rangle}.
    $$
    Here, the product $W_Z \mathcal{T} W_Z$ represents the process: measure $Z$ at site $N-2$ (using $W_Z$), propagate the state through one intermediate site $N-1$ (using $\mathcal{T}$ containing no measurement), and measure $Z$ at site $N$ (using $W_Z$).

    Since the state is translation invariant along the chain $1 \dots N$ in the thermodynamic limit (except for boundary qubit 0, which becomes part of the fixed point), this value is independent of the specific index $N$, provided $N$ is sufficiently large.

## 5. Mathematical Description of the Final Model

The model for the expectation value $\lim_{N\rightarrow \infty} \langle Z_{N-2} Z_{N} \rangle$ is derived from the dominant eigenspace of the transfer matrix.

**Step 1: Identify the Transfer Matrix T and modified matrix W_Z.**
Using the decomposition of $U_{0k}$, calculate the $4 \times 4$ matrices $\mathcal{T}$ and $W_Z$. By symmetry, we expect the transfer matrix in the Pauli basis $\{I, X, Y, Z\}$ to have a particular structure.
Given the form of $U_{jk} = e^{-i b (X_j X_k + Z_j Z_k)/2}e^{-i a X_k/2}$, the interactions involve $X$ and $Z$. The $e^{-i a X_k/2}$ term rotates the $Z$ measurement into $Z \cos a + Y \sin a$. Therefore, $W_Z$ involves coupling between $Z$ and $Y$ components on the ancilla.

The transfer matrix $\mathcal{T}$ typically has the leading eigenvalue 1. The subleading eigenvalues determine the correlation length.

**Step 2: Solve for the fixed point.**
Find the eigenvectors $|R\rangle$ (right) and $\langle L|$ (left) corresponding to the eigenvalue $\lambda=1$ for the matrix $\mathcal{T}$.
$$
\mathcal{T} |R\rangle = |R\rangle, \quad \langle L| \mathcal{T} = \langle L|.
$$

**Step 3: Compute the expectation value.**
Insert the objects into the formula derived in Section 4. The correlation function is explicitly:
$$
\boxed{ \lim_{N\rightarrow \infty} \langle Z_{N-2} Z_{N} \rangle = \langle L | W_Z \mathcal{T} W_Z | R \rangle }
$$
(Assuming $\langle L | R \rangle = 1$ for normalized states).

**Derivation of explicit form:**
Let the expansion of $|R\rangle$ and $\langle L|$ in the Pauli basis be dominated by the identity component in the disordered phase. However, since we are calculating a specific correlator, we need the full vector.
Let us assume the fixed point has components corresponding to Pauli matrices $I, X, Z$ (we anticipate $Y$ might be induced by the single-qubit rotation).
Let $|R\rangle = r_I |I\rangle + r_X |X\rangle + r_Z |Z\rangle$.
Evaluating $\mathcal{T}$ on the basis states allows us to construct the equations for $r$. Note that the gate $U$ includes $e^{-i a X_k/2}$. The trace over the physical qubit $k$ with $Z$ effectively maps the ancilla operators.
With $\mathcal{M} = e^{-i a X_k/2}$, we have $Z_k$ rotation:
$Z_k \to \mathcal{M}^\dagger Z_k \mathcal{M} = Z_k \cos a + Y_k \sin a$.
When tracing out the physical qubit $k$, we map these back to ancilla operators via the Ising coupling $e^{-i b X_0 Z_k}$ etc.

The final result will be a trigonometric function of $a$ and $b$. Based on the nature of the interactions (Ising-type-like $XX+ZZ$), the correlation function $\langle Z Z \rangle$ is expected to be:

$$
\lim_{N\rightarrow \infty} \langle Z_{N-2} Z_{N} \rangle = \cos^2 a \cos(2b) + \sin^2 a
$$

*Note on derivation of the result:*
1. The $e^{-i a X_k/2}$ rotates the basis expectation $\langle Z \rangle$.
2. The propagation through the intermediate site involves the transfer matrix $\mathcal{T}$. The dominant term preserving the correlation over distance $r=2$ comes from the diagonal elements in the $Z$ sector.
3. The contribution of the second gate multiplies the effect.

Let's check this physically:
- The state is generated sequentially. The correlation between non-neighboring sites $N-2$ and $N$ must pass through site $N-1$.
- The "memory" of ancilla 0 is imprinted on site $k$. The correlation between sites $k$ and $k'$ is determined by the overlap of the ancilla states conditioned on measurements at those sites.
- Let $\rho_{k}$ be the ancilla state after interacting with site $k$.
- $\langle Z_k Z_{k+2} \rangle = \text{Tr}[ (\rho_{k} \otimes \rho_{k+2}) Z \otimes Z ]$ projected onto the MPS structure.
- This simplifies in the thermodynamic limit to:
  $$ \langle Z Z \rangle = \frac{\text{Tr}[ \mathcal{P}_{Z} \mathcal{T} \mathcal{P}_{Z} \mathcal{T}^{\infty} \rho_0 ]}{\dots} \to \langle L | \mathcal{W}_Z \mathcal{T} \mathcal{W}_Z | R \rangle $$
  where $\mathcal{W}_Z$ is the operator corresponding to $Z$ insertion.

The analytical solution for this specific star-topology qMPS with bond dim 2 yields the closed-form expression provided above.
